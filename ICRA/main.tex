\begin{abstract}
  Quadruped robots must decide where to step and which leg to move.
  Fixed gait schedules make this problem easier. They also make the
  robot less reactive when safe footholds appear in irregular places.
  We present GaitNet, a greedy footstep planner for this setting. At
  each \corlControlTickMs\,ms control tick, it makes one small
  decision: move one leg to one foothold for a chosen duration, or do
  nothing. A swing lasts
  \corlSwingDurationMin--\corlSwingDurationMax\,s, so GaitNet can
  decide again while another leg is still in the air. Multi-leg
  motion then comes from repeated fast decisions. It does not require
  a gait phase, a library of motion primitives, or a planned contact
  schedule. \changed{At deployment, GaitNet scores every valid cell in
  each leg's terrain mask in one batched pass. It then selects the
  highest-value option among all valid cells and the no-action
  option.} We train the policy with reinforcement learning in NVIDIA
  Isaac Lab. We deploy it on a Unitree Go1 through the Legged
  Perceptive Stack's NMPC/WBC tracking pipeline. \pending{Across
  matched hardware difficulty levels, GaitNet reaches a
  \textbf{\corlHwFullSuccess\%} mean success rate. The scheduled NMPC
  baseline reaches \textbf{\corlHwLPSSuccess\%}.}
\end{abstract}
