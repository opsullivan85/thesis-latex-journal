\begin{abstract}
  Quadruped locomotion on unstructured terrain is fundamentally a
  contact planning problem. Gait schedules that fix which legs swing
  together constrain a robot's ability to react to unplanned terrain.
  We introduce GaitNet, a greedy footstep planner that never plans
  coordination between legs. At each \corlControlTickMs\,ms control
  tick it selects at most one footstep from a set of candidates
  filtered by terrain, or elects to take no action. Because a selected
  swing lasts \corlSwingDurationMin--\corlSwingDurationMax\,s, several
  further decisions occur while a leg is still airborne, and the
  planner may commit a second leg partway through the first leg's
  swing. Concurrency between legs is therefore emergent rather than
  scheduled. Nothing in the action space represents a phase offset or
  a gait primitive. Candidates are drawn from a terrain validity mask
  and locally refined against GaitNet's own learned value function
  through a short projected gradient step before the final selection.
  We train GaitNet with reinforcement learning in NVIDIA Isaac Lab,
  building on simulation results from our preliminary framework, and
  evaluate the resulting system for the first time on a physical
  Unitree Go1 against the Legged Perceptive Stack, a leading
  perceptive NMPC baseline. GaitNet achieves a
  \textbf{\corlHwFullSuccess\%} mean success rate versus
  \textbf{\corlHwLPSSuccess\%} for the baseline across matched terrain
  difficulties.
\end{abstract}
