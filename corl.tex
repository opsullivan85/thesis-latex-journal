\begin{abstract}
  Quadruped locomotion on unstructured terrain is fundamentally a
  contact planning problem, and gait schedules that fix which legs
  swing together constrain a robot's ability to react to unplanned
  terrain. We introduce GaitNet, a greedy footstep planner that
  never reasons about multi-leg coordination directly: at each
  40\,ms control tick it selects at most one footstep from a set of
  terrain-filtered candidates, or elects to take no action. Because
  a selected swing lasts 0.1--0.3\,s, several further decisions occur
  while a leg is still airborne, and the planner may commit a second
  leg mid-swing. Multi-leg concurrency is therefore emergent rather
  than scheduled: nothing in the action space represents a phase
  offset or a gait primitive. Candidates are drawn from a
  terrain-validity mask and locally refined against GaitNet's own
  learned value function via a short projected gradient step before
  the final selection. We train GaitNet with reinforcement learning
  in NVIDIA Isaac Lab and evaluate it in simulation across a range of
  terrain difficulties and commanded velocities, and on a physical
  Unitree Go1 against the Legged Perceptive Stack, a state-of-the-art
  perceptive NMPC baseline, where GaitNet achieves a
  \textbf{XX\%} mean success rate versus \textbf{XX\%} for the
  baseline across matched terrain difficulties.
\end{abstract}
