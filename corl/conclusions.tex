This paper formulated and validated an actively integrated control
framework capable of rigorously generating true dynamic, acyclic
contact sequences in quadrupedal robots by unifying neural
network-driven learning evaluation techniques with resilient,
traditional model-predictive and heuristic controller systems. By
directly adopting \textit{GaitNet}\textemdash a comprehensive
differential reward proxy\textemdash continuous workspace coordinates
were systematically extracted outside of predefined boundaries
without relying on grid mapping implementations standard to
contemporary heuristic combinations.

Fundamentally contrasting discrete sample-generation architectures,
mathematically evaluating these raw continuous candidate spaces
explicitly through iterative projected gradient ascent ensures
maximal utility estimation mapping precisely bounded footstep
trajectories out of un-steppable spaces (holes or unstable
geometries). By effectively discarding the rigid interpolation
boundaries inherent in finite spatial grids, this methodology
successfully decouples footprint evaluation from discrete,
scale-dependent constraints and optimizes actions over an analytic
estimation surface. This effectively harmonizes raw, reactive
continuous environmental metrics with discrete-action logic without
simultaneously forfeiting the proven trajectory safety embedded
within typical model-predictive whole-body control hierarchies.

Our derived architecture inherently supports fully independent,
reactive, non-gaited locomotion strategies alongside conventional
walking or trot phasing dynamically determined by environmental
structure rather than manual coding constraints, comfortably
accommodating simultaneous multi-leg swing phases whenever physically
advantageous to the traverse task. As conclusively demonstrated, the
physical hardware deployment across challenging physical step
structures explicitly validating the Unitree Go1 highlights
compelling, quantifiable improvements in fault-free success rates
across challenging, increasing physical terrain densities when
benchmarked relative to fixed-gait or pure predictive control
baselines, such as the state-of-the-art \textit{Legged Perceptive
Stack}. The unconstrained optimization framework directly addresses
the severe performance regressions typically noted when periodic
routines inevitably misalign with completely randomized or unknown
traversal gaps.

Concluding, our methodology delivers highly empirical evidence
detailing exactly why transitioning past rigid geometry generative
approaches toward inherently iterative, continuous gradient-based
mappings built securely from approximated neural value outputs can
genuinely push multi-legged robotics remarkably closer to universally
robust and dynamic agility.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
While the framework successfully navigated structurally variable
solid surfaces, the implementation remains dependent on robust
proprioceptive and local ground-plane estimations fed into the
current input feature vector $x_g$. Future work will aim to
incorporate direct sensory information processing techniques (such as
  deep integration of localized point clouds or exteroceptive height
  maps filtered through convolutional layers directly tied into the
state inference pipeline) to significantly reduce sensitivity to
external estimation drift.

In addition, we plan to explicitly expand the available action
parameterization beyond localized leg geometry tracking to fully
integrate dynamic localized posture tracking and whole-body
center-of-mass shift optimization. Doing so would empower the
gradient ascent pipeline to systematically adjust dynamic parameters
targeting much steeper multi-level gradients\textemdash spanning
unstructured steep inclines as well as generalized continuous stair
climbing routines\textemdash completely devoid of hard-coded
elevation thresholds or external kinematic heuristics. Ultimately,
structurally accommodating temporal horizon tuning factors inside the
internal projected steps would further elevate precise phase-duration
tuning directly parallel to spatial footprint selection.
