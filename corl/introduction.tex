\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Legged robots hold significant promise for enabling mobility in
environments that are inaccessible to wheeled and tracked systems.
Their ability to traverse uneven, unstructured, and unpredictable
terrains\textemdash such as disaster zones, staircases, dense
vegetation, and rocky landscapes\textemdash positions them as key
enablers for future exploration, inspection, and rescue missions.
Unlike wheeled rovers, which are fundamentally limited by the continuous
support surface required for traction, quadrupedal systems can effectively
decouple their path of motion from the continuous topology of the ground,
selecting only the most viable, discrete footholds across otherwise
impassable terrain.

Realizing this potential requires a robot to continuously and
intelligently decide where and when to place its feet\textemdash a
process known as \textit{contact planning}. Unlike periodic gait
patterns suitable for flat or predictable surfaces, real-world
locomotion often demands dynamic, acyclic, and adaptive footstep
sequences. Even minor errors in contact selection can lead to
instability, joint torque limit violations, or catastrophic failure,
whereas well-chosen footholds enable
efficient, agile, and robust movement.

The generation of such contact plans in real time remains a
formidable challenge. It requires the system to perceive its
surroundings, anticipate the dynamic and kinematic outcomes of
possible actions, and select
feasible contact points within tight temporal constraints (typically
  matching real-time
control frequencies of 50-500 Hz). Existing
methods often fall into one of three categories, fully using
traditional control\textemdash model predictive controllers, PD
controllers, optimization\textemdash using neural networks, or a mix
of the two: hybrid methods. These three methods offer different
trade-offs between deliberative, computationally intensive planning and
fast, reactive control that lacks stability or generalization.

The vision of this research is to develop a hybrid control
architecture that mitigates the trade-offs inherent in these existing
schemas. Specifically, this work
aims to design a system capable of producing non-gaited, dynamic
footstep plans that retain both the rapid responsiveness of learning-based
approaches and the proven reliability of model-based control. The objective
is to provide quadruped robots with the decision-making agility
necessary for navigating complex terrains while maintaining the
stability and robustness demanded by real-world operation on physical hardware.

\subsection{Related Work}

\subsubsection{Traditional Control Approaches}
Traditional implementations for quadruped locomotion commonly rely on explicit
optimization of footstep positions, contact sequences, and timing,
heavily utilizing
Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks
\cite{Kim2019highly-dynamic}. Many variables
like joint torques and ground reaction forces map well to continuous
optimization \cite{Wensing2022Nov}.
However, introducing discrete contact states into these frameworks
generates highly complex Mixed-Integer Quadratic Programs (MIQP)
\cite{Geisert2019contact-planning}.

Deits and Tedrake \cite{deits_footstep_2014} addressed this by
formulating footstep
placement as an MIQP over convex terrain regions, yet their
formulation did not optimize
contact timing and restricted motion to sequential placements.
Subsequent frameworks
incorporated contact scheduling directly. Winkler et al.
\cite{winkler_gait_2018}
optimized both contact duration and ordering, and Aceituno-Cabezas et
al. \cite{aceituno_simultaneous_2019}
integrated footstep positions and timings into a mixed-integer convex
program via
centroidal dynamics. While these methods allow multiple steps to be planned
simultaneously, the computational burden precludes reactive real-time control.
Search-based methods, such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), have also been
explored by Taouil et al. \cite{taouil_non-gaited_2024} to generate non-gaited
sequences, and later refined by Akizhanov et al. \cite{akizhanov_learning_2024}
using learned classifiers for pruning. However, high branching factors keep
the computational intensity too steep for high-frequency dynamic correction.

\subsubsection{End-to-End Neural Networks}
End-to-end learning methods bypass explicit dynamics modeling,
mapping sensor data
directly to joint targets. Zhang et al. \cite{zhang_learning_2023} utilized Deep
Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to achieve highly robust locomotion
generalized across
terrains through curiosity rewards. A
follow-up approach by
Zhang et al. \cite{zhang_novel_2024} implemented heuristically
defined tables to handle
multi-gait transitions. While end-to-end models inherently solve the
mixed-integer
contact problem, they typically lack formal stability guarantees,
require extensive
data to train, and struggle to generalize reliably outside their
curriculum distribution.

\subsubsection{Hybrid Methods and Learned Gait Policies}
Hybrid architectures merge learning components with model-based
execution to balance
tractability and guaranteed stability. Several methods focus on
high-level gait modulation.
Shi et al. \cite{shi_terrain-aware_2023} modulate trajectory
parameters for efficiency,
while Xie et al. \cite{xie_glide_2023} train RL policies on
centroidal dynamics given
fixed gait heuristics. Blind proprioceptive approaches further push
robust traversal,
as seen in Duan et al. \cite{duan_sim--real_2022} and Siekmann et al.
\cite{siekmann_blind_2021}
for stair climbing, while Lee et al. \cite{lee_learning_2020} extract
terrain features
solely via proprioceptive histories. Other systems explicitly select
gaits via Deep
Q-Networks (DQN) \cite{da_learning_2020}, neural phase integrators
\cite{yang_fast_2021},
and offline gait libraries integrated into high-frequency MPC
\cite{sun_online_2024}.
Although these models generate agile locomotion, they rely strongly
on periodic gaits
and struggle mapping truly acyclic footprint geometry over severely
unstructured gaps.

\subsubsection{Learning-Based Footstep Planners}
Contrasting pure gait modulation, explicit data-driven footstep
planners couple foothold
decision-making with MPC solvers. Early architectures like
FootstepNet \cite{gaspard_footstepnet_2024},
DeepLoco \cite{peng_deeploco_2017}, and DeepGait
\cite{tsounis_deepgait_2020} laid the groundwork
but frequently localized to bipeds or relied on fixed cyclic stepping
patterns. Frameworks like
RLOC \cite{gangapurwala_rloc_2022}, Omar et al.
\cite{omar_fast_2022}, and Villarreal et al.
\cite{villarreal_fast_2019} leverage convolutional terrain inputs
combined with fixed or periodic routines.

Recent expansions into multi-contact reasoning have evaluated terrain
curvature for foot positioning \cite{tolomei_learning_2025}, hexapod
stepping policies \cite{chen_gait_2024}, and learned 3D foothold cost
maps \cite{meduri_deepq_2021}, yet invariably
operate under predefined gait periods. Greedy and heuristic-based
implementations, such as GH-QP \cite{gao_global_2024}, Dubins car
heuristics \cite{zucker_optimization_2011}, and cost-search
algorithms \cite{kalakrishnan_learning_2011}, present efficiency
improvements but similarly falter outside static cyclic motions.
Learned steppability terrain maps
\cite{asselmeier_steppability-informed_2024, omar_safesteps_2023}
inform explicit foothold placements but do not intrinsically yield
sequential dynamic paths.

Consequently, while convolutional evaluators
\cite{bratta_contactnet_2024} successfully extract non-gaited maps
via greedy grid selection, they suffer heavily from coarse spatial
resolutions and constrain execution to sequential, one-leg-at-a-time
motions, severely restricting the robot's kinetic envelope.

\subsection{Motivation and Contributions}
Despite this progress, a clear gap remains in achieving real-time,
non-gaited contact planning.
By depending heavily on highly discretized grid approximations for
sampling \cite{bratta_contactnet_2024}
or rigid cyclic timing constraints,
current hybrid methods trade spatial
accuracy and continuous tempo variation for computational tractability.

This motivates our core inquiry: \textit{Can a continuous
  optimization pipeline utilizing gradient
  ascent across an approximated neural value surface generate true
  acyclic, reactive gaits without
relying on discrete spatial sampling or rigid contact schedules?}

Our proposed approach bypasses discrete spatial sampling entirely. We
introduce a novel
actor-critic architecture, \textit{GaitNet}, to synthesize value
estimations mapped across the
continuous physical workspace. Operating exclusively over the
projected continuous space rather
than a discretized grid, GaitNet iteratively applies projected
gradient ascent to shift
target footsteps radially toward local reward maxima, naturally
repelling footprints out of
dangerous regions (e.g. gaps or holes).

Our primary contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A greedy neural footstep planner explicitly driven by
    continuous projected gradient ascent on theoretical reward
    limits, functionally omitting spatial evaluation grid constraints.
  \item The simultaneous generation of true dynamic, acyclic gaits
    capable of multi-leg concurrent swing phases governed exclusively
    by environmental necessity rather than hard-coded sequences.
  \item Extensive quantitative real-world validation of continuous
    footprint optimization utilizing a physical Unitree Go1 platform,
    benchmarked directly against leading heuristic trajectory planners (OCS2).
\end{enumerate}
