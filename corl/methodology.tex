\subsection{System Architecture}
\label{subsec:methodology-system-overview}

This work presents a control framework for quadruped robots capable
of generating dynamic, acyclic gaits in challenging environments
without requiring a predefined periodic schedule. The framework uses
a hierarchical architecture that separates high-level footstep
decisions from low-level kinematic tracking. At each control
tick, a terrain height scan is thresholded and dilated into a
per-leg validity mask, a small set of candidate footsteps is drawn
within that mask and locally refined against GaitNet's learned value
function, and the single best candidate, or a no-action
option, is selected for the entire robot and passed to a
low-level tracking controller for execution via model predictive
control and whole-body feedback. GaitNet's footstep action is
consumed identically regardless of which controller executes it.
In simulation, the tracking controller is the MIT Mini-Cheetah
Controller \cite{di_mini_cheetah_2020}, structured in python by
\cite{zhuang_rl_mpc_locomotion_2025}. On hardware, GaitNet instead
supplies footstep targets directly to the Legged Perceptive Stack's
own NMPC/Whole-Body Control (WBC) pipeline
\cite{leggedperceptive}, in place of that framework's
native gait scheduler.

Two constraints are enforced directly in the validity mask, before
GaitNet ever sees a candidate. First, a leg already in its swing
phase is excluded from candidate generation, since it cannot be
redirected mid-swing. Second, a new step is permitted only while at
least \numberstringnum{\corlMinContactLegs} legs remain in ground
contact, which caps concurrent swing at
\numberstringnum{\corlMaxConcurrentSwingLegs} legs. This is a
deliberate feasibility constraint, not an incidental limitation, and
every result in \autoref{sec:results} operates under it. The validity
mask itself is computed from a thresholded height scan, dilated by
\numberstringnum{\corlMaskDilationRounds} rounds of max-pooling to
conservatively account for the physical extent of the robot's foot.

\subsection{High-Rate Action Selection}
\label{subsec:methodology-high-rate-selection}

GaitNet is evaluated at \corlControlHz\,Hz, a \corlControlTickMs\,ms
control tick, and never represents coordination between legs
directly. At most one footstep is committed per tick, for one leg,
and the rest of the robot is left untouched until its own turn. A
selected swing lasts between \corlSwingDurationMin\,s and
\corlSwingDurationMax\,s, so \numberstringnum{\corlMinTicksPerSwing}
to \numberstringnum{\corlMaxTicksPerSwing} further decision ticks
elapse while that leg is still airborne. GaitNet may therefore commit
a second leg mid-swing of the first, subject only to the
contact-count constraint above. Concurrency between legs is an
emergent consequence of re-deciding faster than a swing completes,
not a behavior the architecture was given machinery to plan. Whether
the policy learns to anticipate future ticks and implicitly
coordinate legs through this repeated re-selection is a question of
what reinforcement learning discovers, not something encoded in the
action space itself. The simplicity of a single-leg, single-tick
action space is what makes acyclic, dynamic gait generation tractable
at the rates of the control loop.

\subsection{GaitNet Formulation}
\label{subsec:methodology-gaitnet-architecture}

GaitNet is a single-network architecture comprised of a robot state
encoder, a footstep encoder, a shared trunk, and two output heads
specific to each task. The first head predicts a scalar value
logit used to rank a candidate. The second predicts the swing
duration to use if that candidate is selected. GaitNet evaluates
candidates conditioned on a one-hot leg encoding (including a
no-action option) and the current tracking state $\mathbf{x_g}$:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x_g} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathbf p_{b,xy} &
    \mathbf r_{w,z} &
    \mathbf v_b &
    \mathbf \omega_b &
    \mathbf u &
    \mathbf g &
    \mathbf c
  \end{bmatrix}^T
  \label{eq:gaitnet-x}
\end{equation}

Here $\mathbf p_{b,xy} \in \mathbb{R}^{\corlFootPosDim}$ is the $xy$
position of all four end-effectors in the base frame, and
$\mathbf r_{w,z}$ is the height of the robot's center of mass in the
world frame. $\mathbf v_b \in \mathbb{R}^{\corlVelDim}$ and
$\mathbf \omega_b \in \mathbb{R}^{\corlAngVelDim}$ are the robot's
linear and angular velocity in the root frame.
$\mathbf u = [v_x, v_y, \omega_z]$ is the commanded velocity.
$\mathbf g \in \mathbb{R}^{\corlGravityDim}$ is the direction of
gravity in the body frame, and
$\mathbf c \in \{0,1\}^{\corlContactDim}$ is the per-foot contact
state. Together these total \corlStateDim{} dimensions. Notably, the
inclusion of $\mathbf \omega_b$ distinguishes this formulation from
that of ContactNet \cite{bratta_contactnet_2024}, which omits angular
velocity from its state representation. We add it to improve
performance in situations with high rotational velocities.

The network implements a middle-fusion style feature combinator
\cite{feng2021deep}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Robot State Encoder}: The \corlStateDim-dimensional
    state vector is processed by a
    \numberstringnum{\corlStateEncoderLayers}-layer Multi-Layer
    Perceptron (MLP) with a hidden dimension of
    \corlStateEncoderHidden{} and ReLU activations, producing a
    latent state representation of fixed dimension.
  \item \textbf{Footstep Encoder}: Each candidate's spatial
    coordinates are embedded by a
    \numberstringnum{\corlFootstepEncoderLayers}-layer feedforward
    network with a hidden size of \corlFootstepEncoderHidden{} and
    ReLU activations. The no-action option is not spatial at all. It
    is routed to a separate, learned embedding vector rather than
    through this encoder.
  \item \textbf{Shared Trunk}: The latent state and the candidate
    embedding are concatenated and passed into a
    \numberstringnum{\corlTrunkLayers}-layer shared trunk, with layer
    normalization applied after each linear mapping.
  \item \textbf{Output Heads}: A value head outputs the unnormalized
    logit $\hat{V}$ used for ranking. A duration head outputs
    $t_{\text{swing}}$, scaled by a sigmoid into
    $(\corlSwingDurationMin, \corlSwingDurationMax)$\,s.
\end{itemize}

In the configuration evaluated in this paper, candidates carry no
learned cost input. The footstep evaluation network used in prior
work \cite{Sullivan2025} to score candidates in advance has been
removed entirely, and GaitNet's own value head is the only source of
ranking information. This choice is motivated directly by an
ablation in \cite{Sullivan2025} in which zeroing that cost input
\emph{improved} survival rate over supplying it, suggesting the
precomputed heuristic constrained exploration rather than
accelerating it.

\subsection{Candidate Generation and Refinement}
\label{subsec:methodology-gaitnet-gradient-ascent}

For each leg, \numberstringnum{\corlCandidatesPerLeg} candidate
positions are drawn from a Gaussian distribution
($\sigma = \corlCandidateStd$\,m) centered on the foot's nominal
position. A sample that already lands in a valid, steppable
cell of that leg's terrain mask is kept at its continuous, unsnapped
coordinates. A sample that lands outside the valid region is instead
projected onto the nearest valid cell via a precomputed Euclidean
distance transform. Because GaitNet's value head is differentiable
with respect to a candidate's spatial coordinates, each of the
\numberstringnum{\corlCandidatesPerLeg} samples is then refined for
\numberstringnum{\corlGradientAscentIters} iterations of gradient
ascent on $\hat V$ at a learning rate of $\corlGradientAscentLR$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{f}_{k+1} = \Pi_{\mathcal{C}} \left( \mathbf{f}_k + \alpha
  \nabla_{\mathbf{f}} \hat{V}(\mathbf{x}_g, \mathbf{f}_k; \theta) \right)
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ is the identity whenever its argument already
lies in a valid cell of the mask $\mathcal{C}$, and otherwise projects
it onto the nearest valid cell using the same precomputed distance
transform used for initialization. Gradient ascent therefore proceeds
over continuous candidate coordinates. The discrete mask, at a
resolution of \corlMaskResolutionCm\,cm, projects the Candidates
into a valid cell only when they would otherwise land in a void or
unsteppable region.

After refinement, GaitNet evaluates the fixed no-action embedding
alongside the
$\corlCandidatesPerLeg \times \corlNumLegs = \corlTotalCandidatesDeploy$
refined candidates (\numberstringnum{\corlCandidatesPerLeg} per leg,
\numberstringnum{\corlNumLegs} legs) and selects the single option
with the highest value. If no-action wins, or if a leg's mask has no
valid candidates, no footstep is issued that tick.

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning Framework}
\label{subsec:methodology-gaitnet-training}

Because identifying optimal footsteps depends on temporal horizons
containing discrete transitions in the contact state $\mathbf{c}$,
generating stable, labeled ground-truth data for supervised
regression is prohibitively difficult. GaitNet is instead trained
with reinforcement learning, using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
\cite{Schulman_proximal_2017}.

The actor network is GaitNet itself. The critic is a smaller,
separately sized network that scores each candidate through its own
state encoder, footstep encoder, and trunk, then combines the
scores for each option across all sixteen options with a small combining
MLP to produce a single estimate of state value. It does not share
weights with the actor and omits the duration head. Training uses a
fixed candidate set rather than the refinement procedure above. For
each leg, \numberstringnum{\corlTrainCandidatesPerLeg} candidates are
sampled uniformly at random from the valid terrain mask (plus one
no-action option, \corlTrainTotalCandidates{} candidates total), and
the actor's logits over this set define a categorical
distribution. Refinement based on gradients is used only at
deployment time, applied to this same trained value head. Training
and deployment therefore draw candidates from different
distributions, which we treat as an intentional design choice rather
than an oversight, since it lets the value head be learned as a
general scorer rather than overfit to the refinement trajectory.

The duration output for the selected candidate is treated as a
Normal distribution with a fixed standard deviation of
\corlDurationExploreStd\,s, concentrating exploration on the discrete
action choice rather than timing. The total log probability of an
action is the sum of the categorical log probability of the selected
candidate and the normal log probability of its sampled duration.
Reward terms balance velocity tracking against stability and a small
per-step action penalty ($\corlActionPenalty$) that discourages
unnecessary stepping. Termination on falling or on stepping into
invalid terrain carries a $\corlTerminationPenalty$ penalty. Full
reward and termination tables, command sampling ranges, and PPO
hyperparameters follow the same setup detailed in \cite{Sullivan2025}.
The model is trained in
NVIDIA Isaac Lab \cite{mittal_orbit_2023}, with the physics simulation
and policy running on GPU and the MPC controllers for each robot
running in parallel on CPU, for \corlTrainingIterations{} iterations
across \corlParallelEnvs{} parallel environments, with a curriculum
over terrain difficulty that gradually increases the proportion of
unsteppable terrain as training progresses \cite{Sullivan2025}.
