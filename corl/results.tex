\subsection{Physical Robot Experiments and Baseline Comparison}
\label{subsec:results-baseline-comparison}

To evaluate GaitNet's continuous optimization strategy in strictly
unstructured real-world scenarios rather than simplified simulation
loops, a robust baseline method is required. We benchmark performance
against the \textit{Legged Perceptive Stack} framework
\cite{leggedcontrol}. This state-of-the-art framework employs
Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) and Whole-Body Control
(WBC) routines built atop the OCS2 solver, an open-source control
library engineered to navigate complex terrain through rigidly
scheduled phase optimization trajectories.

A custom physical testing environment was constructed manually to
mirror the variable terrain capabilities evaluated during the primary
simulation benchmarking (\autoref{fig:figure-test-environment}).
Hardware experiments were exclusively conducted utilizing the
commercially available Unitree Go1 quadruped platform. The physical
terrain architecture comprises serialized narrow strips of rigid
wooden ground interspersed with open gaps varying pseudo-randomly in
width. We characterize the environment’s inherent traversal
difficulty analytically as the percentage area of "unsteppable"
terrain spanning the linear tracks (ranging systematically from $0\%$
flat-ground control tests to $40\%$ hazardous gap densities).

Because standard metrics such as tracking error can be heavily skewed
by controller calibration on the hardware, we evaluate overall
mission performance metrics designed to reflect operational autonomy
linearly: success rate (completing traversal from endpoint A to
  endpoint B without the robot’s base contacting the ground or
significantly drifting off course), total accumulated steps taken per
run, and overall traversal time measured in seconds across 25
independent physical trials per algorithm.

The evaluated algorithms span the following conditions to assess
specific architectural components:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Legged Perceptive Stack Baseline:} The
    state-of-the-art, open-source model predictive controller
    governed via defined heuristic gait timing arrays \cite{leggedcontrol}.
  \item \textbf{GaitNet-Single:} An explicitly constrained ablation
    of GaitNet operating identically to the primary neural-network
    but functionally restricted to allowing only one unique leg in
    the swing phase at any given moment.
  \item \textbf{GaitNet-Trot:} A constrained ablation of GaitNet
    enforcing strict paired lateral synchronicity (e.g., only
    opposing diagonal legs are permitted to swing concurrently)
    matching typical heuristic gaits.
  \item \textbf{GaitNet (Full):} The proposed unlimited acyclic
    footprint generation model relying solely on the projected ascent
    reward estimates to orchestrate any physically permissible swing
    configuration at will.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout the experiments, an execution sequence is rigorously
classified as successful exclusively if the robot effectively
completes the entire traversal length without collapsing or
accidentally planting a foot within the removed, structurally invalid
terrain sections.

\subsection{Physical Locomotion Performance}
\label{subsec:physical_locomotion_performance}

\autoref{fig:data-experiments-survival-curr} summarize empirical
hardware results characterizing GaitNet (including the two restricted
architectural ablations) compared symmetrically alongside the OCS2
NMPC baseline method traversing flat ground up to $40\%$ unsteppable
gap densities.

GaitNet's fully unconstrained performance strongly outperforms both
the rigid Legged Perceptive Stack and its internal fixed-gait
ablations. This unconstrained autonomy underscores the vital
operational advantage of dynamic, acyclic, structurally independent
spatial optimization over pure heuristic stepping. The unconstrained
system’s capacity to intuitively coordinate independent multi-leg
motions while also adopting dynamic continuous spatial timing
schedules concurrently minimizes the total gross number of physical
steps required throughout the trial distance. The optimization
consistently isolates stable landing spots deep within the valid
terrain configurations.

This performance divergence is exceptionally prominent when
evaluating the trajectories through high-difficulty tracking
intervals ($30$-$40\%$ gap densities). Under these severe
constraints, purely restrictive trot timing structures or single-leg
pacing methodologies actively penalize forward progress by enforcing
sub-optimal and mechanically unsound footprints that frequently
overlap with the gaps in the test structure, leading to joint torque
saturation or outright physical system collapse.

Post-run observational analysis emphasizes that inflexible phase step
schedules universally trap the quadruped in geometrically impossible
demands. Attempting to force a trot across randomized terrain
necessitates impossibly accurate stride length variations decoupled
from the speed commands. In stark contrast, GaitNet’s integrated
greedy framework, executing entirely devoid of predefined cyclic
periodicity bounds, aggressively capitalizes on isolated continuous
steps predicting peak value. Consequently, the projected points
automatically shift out of the hazardous coordinates and anchor
themselves securely on stable surfaces deep inside the robot’s
actionable workspace.

\subsection{Simulation Ablations}
\label{subsec:results-simulation-ablations}

Alongside the culminating physical evaluations, rigorous standalone
simulation ablations were compiled quantitatively to substantiate
several distinct internal architecture decisions. Principally, this
includes proving the necessity of the concurrent auxiliary dynamic
swing duration networks and the explicit magnitude of predefined
action cost penalties implemented during the proximal policy
optimization phase. To maintain consistent validity, numerical
datasets were accrued traversing fundamentally identical randomized
terrain distributions spanning exactly 200 distinct simulated
episodes, tracking how the continuous spatial variables manifest
internally before being piped to the lower-level MPC layers.

An initial ablation removed the dynamic swing duration head spanning
GaitNet, forcibly constraining the network to operate under rigid,
uniform temporal phase transitions irrespective of the physical step
length requested. Comparing this flat-timing formulation against
GaitNet's dynamic velocity-governed predicted durations unveiled
critical insight into stability. Incorporating the duration head
effectively enabled the robot's simulated body to subconsciously slow
its tracking stepping dynamics whenever traversing heavily degraded,
sparse gaps—an action vital to maintaining body equilibrium—while
concurrently allowing incredibly rapid, efficient strides when
crossing wide, secure geometric blocks. Isolating the inference task
back into a static single-variable spatial puzzle decoupled from
temporal adjustments forced the flat-rate network to routinely
overshoot dynamic targets out of momentum over wide gaps, ultimately
yielding an unrecoverable $+15\%$ surge in physical tracking failures
and significant, dangerous threshold violations of the joint torque
safety bounds.

A secondary simulation run measured the internal optimization
landscape related specifically to the reward design of an explicit
sub-step action cost penalty. Mathematically, applying a persistent
action penalty multiplier fixed at $-0.05$ to any deliberate
trajectory choice deviating from the pure "no-action" decision proved
indispensable in suppressing mathematically jittery or redundant
sub-steps common in unstructured terrain climbing. Empirical tracking
demonstrated that a model formulation lacking this penalization
consistently evolved behaviors characterized by rapid, unnecessary
stepping oscillations (e.g., executing five or six cramped steps
  consecutively merely to complete essentially the equivalent kinetic
translation achievable smoothly via two extended strides). Operating
without intrinsic energetic limitations intrinsically favors
inefficient, high-frequency oscillations. By explicitly embedding
continuous penalty weights directly into the logit, GaitNet
systematically weeds out these unstable locomotion loops, effectively
normalizing the spatial gradients evaluated during the internal $4
\times 4$ projection tracks. Factoring in this minute penalty
analytically resolved into a sweeping $28\%$ measured reduction in
mechanical energy dissipated to useless non-forwarding motion metrics
throughout continuous continuous stride testing cycles.
