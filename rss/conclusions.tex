\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented GaitNet, a footstep planner that is given no gait and
that separates choosing a step from executing it. GaitNet picks one
step per tick from sampled safe footholds, and an execution layer
makes each controller land that step where and when GaitNet meant.
Trained on one controller and run with its weights frozen on OCS2,
with terrain from a simulated depth camera, GaitNet matches OCS2's own
trot on sparse voids and succeeds more often on dense voids and at
faster commands. Swinging two legs at once, comparing candidates, and
refitting the IDM for OCS2 each raise its success, the IDM most. Separating what to
step on from how a controller lands the foot there thus appears to be
a practical way to reuse a learned footstep planner across
controllers.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\pending{All results are in simulation; GaitNet has not yet run on a
Unitree Go1.} Within simulation, we moved GaitNet between only one pair
of controllers, and the execution layer was tuned with the same
policy. We have not run GaitNet on OCS2 with a simple hand-made
correction of the landing shift in place of the learned IDM, which
would show how much of the gain needs learning at all. Refitting the
IDM requires logging steps on each new
controller first; adapting it online, in the spirit of
\cite{xie_fada_2026}, could remove that step.

The evidence for GaitNet's design is also incomplete. On OCS2, the
per-candidate network, trained with the same recipe, falls behind
GaitNet on dense voids, but this comparison changes the candidate
mixer and the history at once, and the two have not been ablated
separately. All OCS2 results also come from one checkpoint per
network; GaitNet's is from an unseeded run, \pending{and seeded runs of
the final setting are still training}.

Finally, our tests are mild in several ways. Each trial covers only a
\corlOcsTrackM\,m track (Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-setup}). Commands stay at or below \corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s, and GaitNet was
trained only up to \corlCmdVxMax\,m/s. At \corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids,
GaitNet and OCS2's own trot succeed in at most half of the trials.
GaitNet also chooses from a rule-based mask of safe cells, and
reading depth or height maps directly could help where the rules are
too strict or too loose.
