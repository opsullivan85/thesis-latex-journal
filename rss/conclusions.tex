\label{sec:conclusion}

\looseness=-1 We presented CADENCE, a learned step planner with a small
action space for model-based controllers. Every
\corlControlTickMs\,ms, it picks one of at most \corlTotalCandidates{}
choices, no step, or one leg's step to a safe foothold, and
predicts a swing time. Nothing in training prescribed a gait, yet the
policy we analyzed developed an acyclic gait, with no fixed period or
stepping order, that still paired diagonal legs. In trials across varied terrain (26\% to 49\% voids) and speeds (0.1\,m/s and 0.2\,m/s), and with only its IFM
refitted to run on OCS2's NMPC, CADENCE succeeded in
\corlBenchFull\% of trials, against \corlBenchTrot\% for
OCS2-Trot and \corlBenchStaticWalk\% for OCS2-Single. At \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids,
CADENCE succeeded in \corlBenchFullDense\% of trials against OCS2-Trot's \corlBenchTrotDense\%. Retrained to swing one leg at a time, it
reached only \corlBenchSingle\%, and no simpler footstep correction lifted CADENCE
above OCS2-Trot. The baselines were OCS2's untuned gaits, acyclicity was measured on one earlier run of one policy,
and the refit needed steps logged on the new controller. 

\looseness=-1 Future work will move CADENCE to additional
model-based controllers, measure how few logged steps the refit needs,
and test whether relogging with the deployed IFM
\citep{christiano_transfer_2016} or online adaptation
\citep{xie_fada_2026} reduces the remaining landing error. We will also
extend the candidates to stairs and slopes and compare with gait-free planners.
