\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\looseness=-1 We presented CADENCE, a learned step planner that, every
\corlControlTickMs\,ms, makes one of at most \corlTotalCandidates{}
discrete choices for an unchanged model-based controller (no step, or one
leg's step to a safe foothold) and predicts a swing time. Trained on a
convex MPC with no gait schedule or gait reward, the policy we analyzed
developed an acyclic gait that still paired diagonal legs. With only its
IFM refitted, CADENCE ran frozen on OCS2 and succeeded in
\corlBenchFull\% of benchmark trials, against \corlBenchTrot\% for
OCS2's trot and \corlBenchStaticWalk\% for its static walk, with a lead
over the trot of 30 points at \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids.

\looseness=-1 The non-learned baselines were OCS2's untuned stock gaits,
acyclicity was measured on one earlier run of one policy, and the
transfer needs steps logged on the new controller. Future work
will move CADENCE to further model-based controllers, measure how few
logged steps the refit needs, and test whether relogging
with the deployed IFM \citep{christiano_transfer_2016} or online
adaptation \citep{xie_fada_2026} reduces the remaining landing error. We
will also extend the candidates to stairs and slopes, and compare with
tuned gaits and gait-free planners.
