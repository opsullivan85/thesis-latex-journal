\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% 2026-11-04 flow rewrite: plainer words, less repetition; same facts, numbers and \verify items.

\looseness=-1 Trained on a convex MPC, GaitNet ran frozen on OCS2 with
only its IDM refitted. On the benchmark, it beat OCS2's own trot,
most on the densest voids, and kept most of its success from the
training controller, but it failed on both controllers without the IDM.
All results are in simulation, on one pair of controllers
\pending{(GaitNet has not yet run on a Unitree Go1)}, and the lead and
margin were tuned with the same planner. Only one of GaitNet's
\corlNumSeedsGaitNet{} training seeds ran on OCS2, while the
per-candidate network's four seeds spread over 14 percentage points, so
which network transfers better is open. In its successful trials,
GaitNet almost always swung diagonal pairs, on its own timing, and we
did not log its failed trials, so why GaitNet-Diagonal lost success
mainly in the hardest cell remains open. We have not compared the
refitted IDM with the training controller's IDM or a constant or
speed-dependent offset per leg, a comparison that would show how much of
the gain needs learning. Nor have we measured landing errors next to
voids, where the candidate margin must cover what the IDM's inputs do
not predict
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:idm-theory}). Our tests are mild, with speeds up to
\corlSweepSpeedMax\,m/s and noise-free depth images, and the
benchmark's success criterion does not penalize a robot that stops
halfway, unlike the sweep's. Future work will test whether fewer logged
steps \citep{xie_fada_2026} or relogging with the deployed IDM
\citep{christiano_transfer_2016} cheapens the refit.
