\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Trained on a convex MPC and run frozen on OCS2 with a simulated depth
camera, with only its inverse footstep model refitted, GaitNet matches OCS2's own trot on sparse voids
and succeeds more often on dense voids; without the IDM, it loses that
lead. The analysis of Sec.~\ref{subsec:idm-theory} says what the IDM can do: it
removes the part of the landing error that its inputs predict, exactly
only under the conditions of Assumption~\ref{as:idm-exact}, and
otherwise with a bias, toward the mean logged landing in a
linear-Gaussian model.

The evidence is limited. All results are in simulation
\pending{(GaitNet has not yet run on a Unitree Go1)}, on one pair of
controllers, with an execution layer tuned with the same policy and one
checkpoint per network; \pending{the other seeds have not been tested
on OCS2}. On this benchmark, we have not replaced the OCS2 IDM with the
training controller's IDM or a simple hand-made correction, such as a
constant or speed-dependent offset per leg, which would show how much of
the gain needs refitting and how much needs learning, nor measured
GaitNet's landing errors next to voids, which Remark~\ref{rem:idm-numbers}
leaves open. GaitNet is not given a gait, but we have not measured which
legs it swings together on OCS2; it may have learned a trot-like pairing
with free timing and footholds. Fewer logged steps \citep{xie_fada_2026}
or relogging with the deployed IDM \citep{christiano_transfer_2016}
could cheapen the refit. Our tests are also mild: 3\,m tracks, commands up to
\corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s, and noise-free depth images.
