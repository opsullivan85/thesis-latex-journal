\looseness=-1 To cross level ground with holes, or \emph{voids}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}), a quadruped must decide which leg to lift,
where to place it, for how long, and when. Model-based controllers
\citep{dicarlo_dynamic_2018, ocs2} track such steps well, but they take
them from a cyclic gait: a trot lifts diagonal leg pairs and a static
walk lifts one leg at a time, in a fixed order and on a fixed period,
wherever the safe footholds are. On sparse footholds, a cycle can
therefore call for a step when no safe foothold is in reach.

\looseness=-1 Choosing the gait online instead raises two problems.
First, contact decisions are discrete, so choosing them inside a model
predictive controller (MPC) makes its optimization mixed-integer
\citep{Wensing2022Nov}, whereas learning them end to end replaces the
model-based controller with a policy over joint or foot targets
\citep{lee_learning_2020}. Second, a planner outside the controller only
commands where a foot should land, and the controller lands the foot at
an offset from that command. This offset differs between controllers and
can exceed the margin that keeps a chosen foothold clear of voids, so a
planner trained with one controller can miss safe footholds with
another.

\looseness=-1 Gait-free planners choose contact timing by kinematic
rules or tree search \citep{bjelonic_whole-body_2021,
amatucci_monte_2022, taouil_non-gaited_2024}, or learn it for a given
MPC without choosing footholds \citep{patrizi_rl-augmented_2026}, and
learned hybrids choose the parameters of a cyclic gait, per-foot contact
states, or footholds under a preset timing \citep{yang_fast_2021,
margolis_learning_2021, gangapurwala_rloc_2022,
bratta_contactnet_2024}. At the footstep level,
\citet{akizhanov_learning_2024} correct a nonlinear MPC's contact
targets by hindsight regression, but within a fixed gait. To our
knowledge, no gait-free footstep planner has been moved between two
model-based controllers.

\looseness=-1 We propose CADENCE (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), a learned
planner with a small action space on top of an unchanged model-based
controller. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, a \emph{tick}, it makes one
of at most \corlTotalCandidates{} discrete choices: the \emph{no-step
option}, or one step, a leg and one of \corlTrainCandidatesPerLeg{}
safe cells near its hip, the \emph{candidates}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:action}), plus a swing time. Since a
step starts at the tick it is chosen, the gait is never specified. Its
timing, order, overlap, and pairing emerge from these choices, with no
gait schedule, phase variable, or gait reward. A small transformer, the
\emph{candidate mixer}, compares the options, and an \emph{inverse
footstep model} (IFM), fitted by hindsight regression on logged steps,
turns each chosen foothold into a command expected to land the foot
there. Trained with proximal policy optimization (PPO) on the convex
MPC, CADENCE runs frozen on OCS2's nonlinear MPC (NMPC) and whole-body
controller (WBC), with only the IFM refitted on fewer than 20{,}000 OCS2
steps. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item A learned step planner with a small per-tick action space, on an
    unchanged model-based controller, whose network compares the choices
    with a candidate mixer and a history
    (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-step}--\ref{subsec:method-network}). We do
    not isolate the mixer or the history.
  \item An emergent acyclic gait, measured and compared with a
    model-based controller's own cyclic gaits: on OCS2, CADENCE's gait
    had neither a fixed period nor a fixed order, yet mostly paired
    diagonal legs, and CADENCE succeeded more often than OCS2's trot and
    static walk (Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-transfer}; acyclicity measured on
    one earlier run of one policy).
  \item Transfer between model-based controllers by refitting only the
    IFM (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}
    and~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}), where simpler corrections fell
    short (Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-why}). The refit uses steps logged on
    the new controller, so the transfer is not data-free.
\end{enumerate}

\looseness=-1 Averaged over \corlNumSeedsGaitNet{} training seeds,
CADENCE succeeded in \corlBenchFull\% of OCS2 benchmark trials, against

walk, and its lead over the trot grew to 30 percentage points in both
