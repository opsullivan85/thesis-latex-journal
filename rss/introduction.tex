\looseness=-1 To cross ground with holes, or \emph{voids}, a quadruped must
decide which leg to lift, when, where, and for how long.
Model-based controllers track such steps well, but usually take them
from a fixed gait \citep{dicarlo_dynamic_2018, ocs2}, such as a trot,
which swings diagonal leg pairs in turn on a preset schedule, regardless
of where safe footholds appear.

\looseness=-1 Deciding the gait online raises two problems. First, contact decisions
are discrete, so choosing them inside a model predictive controller
(MPC) makes its optimization mixed-integer \citep{Wensing2022Nov}.
Second, each controller misses commanded footholds in its own way. The
convex MPC we train on overshoots them by up to about twice the margin we keep
between footholds and voids. A planner trained on one controller can
therefore choose a safe foothold and still miss it on another.

\looseness=-1 We propose GaitNet (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), a learned
planner that decides these steps outside the controller, without a gait
schedule. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, a \emph{tick}, it
takes the \emph{no-step option} or picks a leg, a swing time, and
a foothold among a few safe cells near the hip, the \emph{candidates}.
As it may start a step while another leg swings, the order, timing, and
overlap of steps are its own choice. A small transformer, the
\emph{candidate mixer}, compares the candidates, and a one-second
\emph{history} records the robot's motion and each leg's last touchdown
error. An \emph{execution layer} starts and lands these steps. Its only learned part, an \emph{inverse
footstep model} (IFM), turns the chosen foothold into a command
expected to land the foot there. Trained with proximal policy optimization (PPO)
on the convex MPC, with no gait, foot-clearance, or posture reward,
GaitNet then runs frozen on OCS2's nonlinear MPC (NMPC) and whole-body
controller (WBC), with only the IFM refitted. We make three claims:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item GaitNet (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-step}
    and~\ref{subsec:method-network}) chooses a
    model-based controller's steps without a gait schedule. It adds the
    candidate mixer and history to the per-candidate network of
    \citet{Sullivan2025}.
  \item From the convex MPC to OCS2, the frozen GaitNet moved with only
    its IFM refitted on OCS2's logged steps
    (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-idm} and~\ref{subsec:method-deployment});
    with the training controller's IFM, a constant offset per leg, or no
    IFM, it succeeded in at most \corlBenchCtrlMax\%
    (Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-why}). The fit is direct inverse
    modelling \citep{jordan_forward_1992} with hindsight relabelling
    \citep{andrychowicz_hindsight_2017, ghosh_learning_2021}, as in
    \citet{akizhanov_learning_2024}. To our knowledge, no prior work moves
    a gait-free footstep planner between model-based controllers by
    refitting only an IFM.
  \item An analysis of what the IFM can correct
    (Sec.~\ref{subsec:idm-theory}): it states when hindsight regression
    inverts the controller exactly, shows that landing noise biases it
    toward the mean logged landing, and lower-bounds, for exact terrain
    and a point foot, the clearance the \emph{candidate margin}
    leaves. It motivates the IFM's inputs and the margin; neither deployed
    IFM meets its conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\looseness=-1 On OCS2 (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}), GaitNet succeeded
in \corlBenchFull\% against \corlBenchTrot\% for OCS2's own trot and
\corlBenchStaticWalk\% for its static walk, led the trot by 15.0 points
on the densest voids, and at \corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids, beyond its
\corlMaxVoidPct\% curriculum, succeeded in 50\% of sweep trials against the
trot's 12\%.
