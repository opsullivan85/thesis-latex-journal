\label{sec:introduction}

\looseness=-1 To cross ground with holes, or \emph{voids}, a quadruped must
decide which leg to lift, when, where, and for how long.
Model-based controllers track such steps well, but usually take them
from a fixed gait \citep{dicarlo_dynamic_2018, ocs2}, such as a trot,
which swings diagonal leg pairs in turn on a preset schedule, regardless
of where safe footholds appear.

\looseness=-1 Deciding the gait online raises two problems. First, contact decisions
are discrete, so choosing them inside a model predictive controller
(MPC) makes its optimization mixed-integer \citep{Wensing2022Nov}.
Second, each controller misses commanded footholds in its own way. The
convex MPC we train on overshoots them by about the margin we keep
between footholds and voids. A planner trained on one controller may
therefore choose a safe foothold and still miss it on another.

\looseness=-1 We propose GaitNet (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), a learned
planner that decides these steps outside the controller, without a gait
schedule. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, a \emph{tick}, it
takes the \emph{no-step option} or picks a leg, a swing time, and
a foothold among a few safe cells near the hip, the \emph{candidates}.
As it may start a step while another leg swings, the order, timing, and
overlap of steps are its own choice. A small transformer, the
\emph{candidate mixer}, compares the candidates, and a one-second
\emph{history} of the robot's motion shows how the controller executes
its steps. An \emph{execution layer} starts and lands these steps. Its only learned part, an \emph{inverse
footstep model} (IDM), turns the chosen foothold into the command that
lands the foot there. Trained with proximal policy optimization (PPO)
on the convex MPC, with no gait, foot-clearance, or posture reward,
GaitNet then runs frozen on OCS2's nonlinear MPC (NMPC) and whole-body
controller (WBC), with only the IDM refitted. Our
contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \looseness=-1 GaitNet
    (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-step}--\ref{subsec:method-network}), which
    adds the candidate mixer, history, and execution layer to the
    per-candidate network of \citet{Sullivan2025}. That network also
    chose one step per tick, with up to two legs in swing, but scored
    each candidate alone and ran on one controller.
  \item The execution layer (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}
    and~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}), whose IDM is fitted on each
    controller's logged steps by direct inverse modelling
    \citep{jordan_forward_1992} with hindsight relabelling
    \citep{andrychowicz_hindsight_2017, ghosh_learning_2021}, as in
    \citet{akizhanov_learning_2024}. We show that the IDM can remove
    only the landing error that its inputs predict, so the
    \emph{candidate margin} around voids must cover the rest
    (Sec.~\ref{subsec:idm-theory}).
\end{enumerate}

\looseness=-1 On OCS2, in \corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} trials per run on
shared Gazebo worlds (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}), GaitNet succeeded in \corlBenchFull\%
against \corlBenchTrot\% for OCS2's own trot, and in
\corlBenchFullDense\% against \corlBenchTrotDense\% at the densest,
\corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids. It came within five percentage points of
its success on the convex MPC, but without the IDM it failed on both
controllers. In successful trials, it almost always swung diagonal leg
pairs, like the trot, but on its own timing. The per-candidate network's four training seeds averaged
about the trot's success but spread too widely to rank GaitNet against
it. All results are in simulation, on one pair of controllers.
