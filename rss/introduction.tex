\looseness=-1 To cross level ground with holes, or \emph{voids}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}), a quadruped must decide which leg to lift,
where to place it, for how long, and when. Model-based controllers
\citep{dicarlo_dynamic_2018, ocs2} track such steps well, but they take
them from a cyclic gait: a trot lifts diagonal leg pairs and a static
walk lifts one leg at a time, in a fixed order and on a fixed period,
wherever the safe footholds are. On sparse footholds, a cycle can
therefore call for a step when no safe foothold is in reach.

\looseness=-1 Choosing the gait online raises two problems. First,
contact decisions are discrete, so choosing them inside a model
predictive controller (MPC) makes its optimization mixed-integer
\citep{Wensing2022Nov}, whereas learning them end to end replaces the
model-based controller with a policy over joint targets
\citep{lee_learning_2020}. Second, a planner outside the controller only
commands where a foot should land, and the closed-loop map from command
to landing differs between controllers. The convex MPC we train on
overshoots the command by up to \corlOvershootFastMax\,cm, about twice
the \corlMarginCm\,cm margin our footholds keep from voids, whereas OCS2
lands an uncorrected command \verify{\corlOcsLandRmsNoIdm\,cm} RMS away on flat
ground. A planner trained on one controller can hence miss a safe
foothold on another.

\looseness=-1 Gait-free planners search contact timing inside the
controller's optimization \citep{bjelonic_whole-body_2021,
amatucci_monte_2022, taouil_non-gaited_2024, patrizi_rl-augmented_2026},
and learned hybrids tune parameters of a cyclic gait or learn footholds
under a fixed timing \citep{yang_fast_2021, margolis_learning_2021,
gangapurwala_rloc_2022, bratta_contactnet_2024}. The per-candidate
network of \citet{Sullivan2025} generates an acyclic gait but ran only
on its training controller, and \citet{akizhanov_learning_2024} correct
a nonlinear MPC's contact targets by hindsight regression within a fixed
gait. To our knowledge, no gait-free footstep planner has been moved
between two model-based controllers.

\looseness=-1 We propose GaitNet (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), which keeps
the name of the network of \citet{Sullivan2025} and extends it into a learned
planner with a small action space on top of an unchanged model-based
controller. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, a \emph{tick}, it takes the
\emph{no-step option} or starts one step: one of at most
\corlTotalCandidates{} discrete choices, a leg and one of
\corlTrainCandidatesPerLeg{} safe cells near its hip, the
\emph{candidates} (Fig.~\ref{fig:action}), plus a swing time. Since a
step starts at the tick it is chosen, the gait is never specified. Its
timing, order, overlap, and pairing emerge from these choices, with no
gait schedule, phase variable, or gait reward. A small transformer, the
\emph{candidate mixer}, compares the options, and an \emph{inverse
footstep model} (IFM), fitted by hindsight regression on logged steps,
turns each chosen foothold into a command expected to land the foot
there. Trained with proximal policy optimization (PPO) on the convex
MPC, GaitNet runs frozen on OCS2's nonlinear MPC (NMPC) and whole-body
controller (WBC), with only the IFM refitted on \verify{fewer than 20{,}000} OCS2
steps. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item A small per-tick action space for stepping without a gait
    schedule on an unchanged model-based controller
    (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-step}--\ref{subsec:method-network}). It
    extends the per-candidate network of \citet{Sullivan2025} with the
    mixer and a history, which we do not isolate.
  \item Evidence that the emergent acyclic gait, first generated by
    \citet{Sullivan2025} on its training controller, beats a model-based
    controller's own cyclic gaits: on OCS2, GaitNet's gait had neither a
    fixed period nor a fixed order, yet mostly paired diagonal legs, and
    succeeded more often than OCS2's trot and static walk
    (Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-transfer}; acyclicity measured on one earlier
    run).
  \item Transfer between model-based controllers by refitting only the
    IFM (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}
    and~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}), where simpler corrections fell
    short (Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-why}). The refit uses steps logged on
    the new controller, so the transfer is not data-free.
\end{enumerate}

\looseness=-1 Averaged over \corlNumSeedsGaitNet{} training seeds,
GaitNet succeeded in \corlBenchFull\% of OCS2 benchmark trials, against
\corlBenchTrot\% and \corlBenchStaticWalk\% for OCS2's trot and static
walk, and its lead over the trot grew to 30 percentage points in both
\corlBenchVoidHigh\% cells (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).
