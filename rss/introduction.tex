\looseness=-1 To cross ground with holes, or \emph{voids}, a quadruped must
decide which leg to lift, when, where, and for how long.
Model-based controllers track such steps well, but usually take them
from a fixed gait \citep{dicarlo_dynamic_2018, ocs2}, such as a trot,
which swings diagonal leg pairs in turn on a preset schedule, regardless
of where safe footholds appear.

\looseness=-1 Deciding the gait online raises two problems. First, contact decisions
are discrete, so choosing them inside a model predictive controller
(MPC) makes its optimization mixed-integer \citep{Wensing2022Nov}.
Second, each controller misses commanded footholds in its own way. The
convex MPC we train on overshoots them by up to about twice the margin we keep
between footholds and voids. A planner trained on one controller may
therefore choose a safe foothold and still miss it on another.

\looseness=-1 We propose GaitNet (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), a learned
planner that decides these steps outside the controller, without a gait
schedule. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, a \emph{tick}, it
takes the \emph{no-step option} or picks a leg, a swing time, and
a foothold among a few safe cells near the hip, the \emph{candidates}.
As it may start a step while another leg swings, the order, timing, and
overlap of steps are its own choice. A small transformer, the
\emph{candidate mixer}, compares the candidates, and a one-second
\emph{history} of the robot's motion is meant to show how the controller executes
its steps. An \emph{execution layer} starts and lands these steps. Its only learned part, an \emph{inverse
footstep model} (IDM), turns the chosen foothold into a command
expected to land the foot there. Trained with proximal policy optimization (PPO)
on the convex MPC, with no gait, foot-clearance, or posture reward,
GaitNet then runs frozen on OCS2's nonlinear MPC (NMPC) and whole-body
controller (WBC), with only the IDM refitted. We make three claims:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item A learned planner, GaitNet (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-step}
    and~\ref{subsec:method-network}), can choose a model-based
    controller's steps without a gait schedule. It adds the candidate
    mixer and history to the per-candidate network of
    \citet{Sullivan2025}.
  \item Such a planner can move frozen to a new controller
    when only the IDM is refitted on the new controller (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}
    and~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}). The fit itself is direct inverse
    modelling \citep{jordan_forward_1992} with hindsight relabelling
    \citep{andrychowicz_hindsight_2017, ghosh_learning_2021}, as in
    \citet{akizhanov_learning_2024}; what we add is its use as the
    interface between a frozen planner and a new controller.
  \item We give conditions under which the IDM's fit cancels the landing
    error its inputs predict, and lower-bound, for exact terrain and a point
    foot, the void clearance that the \emph{candidate margin} leaves for the rest
    (Sec.~\ref{subsec:idm-theory}).
\end{enumerate}

\looseness=-1 On OCS2, averaged over \corlNumTrainSeeds{} training seeds of
\corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} trials each on shared Gazebo worlds
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}), GaitNet succeeded in \corlBenchFull\%
against \corlBenchTrot\% for OCS2's own trot, and in
\corlBenchFullDense\% against \corlBenchTrotDense\% on the densest
voids (\corlBenchVoidHigh\%). Across the change of controller and simulator,
it came within five percentage points of its success on the convex MPC,
but without the IDM it failed in most trials on both controllers. In successful trials, when it swung two legs at
once, they were almost always a diagonal pair, as in the trot, but on
its own timing. All results are in simulation, on one pair of controllers.
