\begin{abstract}%
  % 2026-11-04 flow rewrite: plainer words, less repetition; same facts, numbers and \verify items.
  A quadruped crossing ground with holes, or voids, must decide which leg
  to lift, when, where, and for how long. Model-based controllers track
  such steps well but usually follow a fixed gait. Choosing contacts
  inside them makes their optimization mixed-integer. We present
  GaitNet, a learned planner that makes these choices outside the
  controller, with no gait schedule. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, it may
  start one step, choosing the leg, swing time, and safe foothold, even
  while another leg swings. An inverse footstep model (IDM), fitted on
  logged steps, turns the chosen foothold into the command that lands
  the foot there, and a margin around voids must cover the error it
  cannot predict. Trained by reinforcement learning on a convex model
  predictive controller (MPC), GaitNet ran frozen on OCS2's nonlinear MPC
  and whole-body controller with only the IDM refitted. In
  \corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} Gazebo trials per run on shared worlds, with a
  simulated depth camera, it succeeded in \corlBenchFull\% against
  \corlBenchTrot\% for OCS2's own trot, and in \corlBenchFullDense\%
  against \corlBenchTrotDense\% at \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids. It kept
  most of its convex-MPC success of \corlBenchIsaacIdm\% but failed on
  both controllers without the IDM. In successful trials, it almost always
  swung diagonal leg pairs, like the trot, but on its own timing. Restricted to them, it stayed
  only about three percentage points above the trot.%
\end{abstract}
