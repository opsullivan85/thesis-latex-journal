\begin{abstract}%
  A learned footstep planner is trained on top of one low-level
  controller, yet each controller lands a commanded foot with its own
  error, and a miss of a few centimetres can put the foot in a hole.
  We study how to reuse such a planner on a new controller without
  retraining it. GaitNet plans footsteps for a quadruped crossing ground
  with voids and is not given a gait schedule: every
  \corlControlTickMs\,ms it chooses one step (a leg, a foothold among
  sampled safe cells, and a swing time) or no step, and it may start a
  step while another leg is still swinging. An inverse footstep model
  (IDM), fitted by regression on a controller's own logged steps, maps
  the foothold GaitNet wants to the command that lands the foot there;
  it is the only learned part refitted for a new controller. We state
  what this hindsight regression estimates, when it inverts the
  controller's footstep map, how it is biased when it does not in a
  linear-Gaussian case, and how its error
  bounds the chance that a step aimed at a safe cell lands in a void.
  GaitNet is trained with PPO on a convex MPC in Isaac Lab and runs
  frozen on OCS2's nonlinear MPC and whole-body controller in Gazebo,
  with terrain from a simulated depth camera. Over
  0--\corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids, it matches OCS2's own trot on sparse
  voids and succeeds in \corlOcsDenseFull\% of trials against
  \corlOcsDenseTrot\% at 40--\corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids
  (\corlOcsTrialsPerPoint{} trials per point). The frozen GaitNet
  depends on the IDM: without it, its success over the void sweep falls
  from \corlOcsAllFull\% to \corlOcsAllNoIdm\%, and its lead over the trot
  on dense voids disappears.%
\end{abstract}
