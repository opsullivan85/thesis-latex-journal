\begin{abstract}
  Quadruped robots that cross ground with holes must decide, step by
  step, which leg to lift, where to place it, and when. Learned
  footstep planners can make these decisions online, but they are
  trained on one low-level controller, and a controller does not always
  place the foot where it is told; different controllers miss in
  different ways. We present GaitNet, which separates choosing a step
  from executing it. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, GaitNet picks one
  step (a leg, a landing point, and a swing time) or no step from
  sampled safe footholds; it is given no gait. An inverse footstep model
  (IDM), fitted from the controller's own logged steps, then turns
  where GaitNet wants the foot into the command that lands it there. On
  the training controller, this correction alone raises an earlier
  learned planner from \xx\% to \xx\% success at
  \corlSimSpeedFast\,m/s. For a new controller, OCS2's nonlinear MPC and
  whole-body controller in simulation, the IDM is refit on that
  controller's own steps. With the training controller's IDM reused,
  the frozen GaitNet already succeeds in \xx\% [\xx, \xx] of trials on
  held-out worlds with exact terrain, against \xx\% [\xx, \xx] for
  OCS2's own trot, a difference our sample cannot resolve, and in
  \xx\% [\xx, \xx] with a simulated depth camera, on worlds that
  include those used to develop the camera pipeline.
  \pending{Results with the OCS2-fitted IDM will follow.}
\end{abstract}
