\begin{abstract}%
  % 2026-11-04: review R06, R19, R23; author's facts of 2026-11-04.
  Model-based legged controllers usually follow a fixed gait, and a
  footstep planner learned on one controller can miss footholds on
  another. We present GaitNet, a learned planner that chooses the gait
  outside a model-based controller and runs frozen on a second one. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, it may start one
  step, choosing the leg, swing time, and safe foothold, even while
  another leg swings. An inverse footstep model (IFM), fitted on each
  controller's logged steps, turns the foothold into a command expected
  to land the foot there; we analyse which part of the landing error this
  fit can remove. Trained by reinforcement learning on a convex
  model predictive controller (MPC), GaitNet ran on OCS2's nonlinear MPC
  and whole-body controller with only the IFM refitted. In
  \corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} Gazebo trials per training seed, with a
  simulated depth camera, it succeeded in
  \corlBenchFull\% against \corlBenchTrot\% for OCS2's own trot and
  \corlBenchStaticWalk\% for its static walk (\corlBenchFullDense\%
  against \corlBenchTrotDense\% on the densest voids), and in
  \corlBenchIsaacIdm\% on the convex MPC in Isaac. With the
  training controller's IFM, a constant offset per leg, or no IFM, it
  succeeded in at most \corlBenchCtrlMax\%. Without a gait reward,
  \corlGaitDiagPairPct\% of its overlapping swings in successful trials
  paired diagonal legs, as in the trot, but with no fixed period between
  a leg's lift-offs.%
\end{abstract}
