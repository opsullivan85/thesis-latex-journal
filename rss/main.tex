\begin{abstract}%
  % 2026-11-13 refocus (rewrite_decisions.md, update 00:35): small action
  % space and emergent acyclic gait. No Isaac number, no earlier-run number
  % and no gait statistic here: the gait claim is in words only, since its
  % qualifiers (earlier run, one policy, successful trials) do not fit the
  % word budget; the introduction and Sec. 5 carry them. Typed: 20,000
  % (no macro).
  Model-based quadruped controllers usually step on a cyclic gait, which
  lifts the legs in a fixed order on a fixed period, wherever the safe
  footholds are. We present GaitNet, a learned step planner with a small
  action space. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, it picks one of at most
  \corlTotalCandidates{} discrete choices, no step or one leg's step to
  a safe foothold, and predicts a swing time. It sets no joint targets
  and no gait parameters, so the controller still tracks the motion.
  Trained by reinforcement learning on a convex model predictive
  controller (MPC), with no gait schedule or gait reward, only a cost
  per step and at most \numberstringnum{\corlMaxConcurrentSwingLegs} legs in swing, the GaitNet policy we analysed developed
  an acyclic gait, with neither a fixed period nor a fixed stepping order,
  that still paired diagonal legs. An inverse footstep model (IFM), fitted on
  a controller's logged steps, turns each foothold into a command. With
  only the IFM refitted, on \verify{fewer than 20{,}000} steps, GaitNet ran frozen
  on OCS2's nonlinear MPC. In \corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} simulated trials
  per training seed on ground with voids, it succeeded in
  \corlBenchFull\% (mean of \corlNumSeedsGaitNet{} seeds), against
  \corlBenchTrot\% for OCS2's cyclic trot and \corlBenchStaticWalk\% for
  its static walk, and \corlBenchFullDense\% against \corlBenchTrotDense\%
  on the densest voids. Retrained to swing one leg at a time, it
  succeeded in \corlBenchSingle\%. With simpler footstep corrections, it
  reached at most \corlBenchCtrlMax\%.%
\end{abstract}
