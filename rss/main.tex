\begin{abstract}%
  % 2026-11-13 refocus (rewrite_decisions.md, update 00:35): small action
  % space and emergent acyclic gait. No Isaac number, no earlier-run number
  % and no gait statistic here: the gait claim is in words only, since its
  % qualifiers (earlier run, one policy, successful trials) do not fit the
  % word budget; the introduction and Sec. 5 carry them. Typed: 20,000
  % (no macro).
  % 2026-11-13, Overleaf comments (O. Sullivan, M. Agheli): the two longest
  % sentences are split ("We train ... The policy we analysed ..." and the
  % results sentence); the densest-void 43.0 % is named as the trot's; the
  % single-leg and the simpler-correction results each state what they are
  % compared with (CADENCE's own rate; the trot's rate).
  Model-based quadruped controllers usually step on a cyclic gait, which
  lifts the legs in a fixed order on a fixed period, wherever the safe
  footholds are. We present CADENCE, a learned step planner with a small
  action space. Every \corlControlTickMs\,ms, it picks one of at most
  \corlTotalCandidates{} discrete choices, no step, or one leg's step to
  a safe foothold, and predicts a swing time, while the controller still
  tracks the motion. We train CADENCE by reinforcement learning on a
  convex model predictive controller (MPC), with no gait schedule or
  gait reward, only a cost per step and at most
  \numberstringnum{\corlMaxConcurrentSwingLegs} legs in swing. The
  policy we analysed developed an acyclic gait, with neither a fixed
  period nor a fixed stepping order, that still paired diagonal legs. An
  inverse footstep model (IFM), fitted on a controller's logged steps,
  turns each foothold into a command. With only the IFM refitted, on
  fewer than 20{,}000 steps, CADENCE ran frozen on OCS2's
  nonlinear MPC. In simulated trials on ground with voids, it succeeded
  in \corlBenchFull\% (mean of \corlNumSeedsGaitNet{} training seeds),
  against \corlBenchTrot\% for OCS2's cyclic trot and
  \corlBenchStaticWalk\% for its static walk. At \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids, it
  succeeded in \corlBenchFullDense\%, against \corlBenchTrotDense\% for
  the trot. Retrained to swing one leg at a time, it succeeded in
  \corlBenchSingle\% instead of \corlBenchFull\%. With simpler footstep
  corrections, it reached at most \corlBenchCtrlMax\%, below the trot's
  \corlBenchTrot\%.%
\end{abstract}
