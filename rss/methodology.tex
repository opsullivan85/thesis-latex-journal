\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Choosing One Step per Tick}
\label{subsec:method-step}

\looseness=-1 Each tick, GaitNet scores every step that
Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} allows against the no-step option and starts at
most one, while the other legs keep their commands. Beyond these rules,
its action names no gait phase, leg pair, or contact sequence; these
follow from when it starts steps. Vertical terrain rays on a
grid of spacing $h = \corlScanResCm$\,cm around each hip mark a cell safe when the ground under it
lies within reach below the hip, and unsafe when it is a void or the ray
misses. The unsafe region is
then grown by $k = 2$ cells on every side, so
that no unsafe ray lies within the candidate margin $m = kh = \corlMarginCm$\,cm
of a remaining safe cell along the grid axes
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:idm-theory}). No other reach, edge, or kinematic rule
is applied. For each leg that may step,
GaitNet picks \corlTrainCandidatesPerLeg{} safe cells that cover the safe region
(App.~\ref{app:sampler}) and leaves slots empty when a leg has fewer. With the no-step
option, they are a tick's choices.

\subsection{Network}
\label{subsec:method-network}

\looseness=-1 GaitNet has three inputs. The state $\mathbf x_g$ holds the
feet's planar positions relative to the trunk, the base height, the base
linear and angular velocity, the commanded velocity, each leg's
scheduled contact, and projected gravity, all in the trunk frame except
the height. Candidate $i$ is described by $\mathbf q_i$, a one-hot code
of its leg and its offset from the hip. The history $\mathbf h$ covers
the last \corlHistSeconds\,s of base
velocities, foot positions relative to the hips from the joint encoders,
scheduled contacts, and each leg's last \emph{touchdown error}, the
foot's position at its scheduled touchdown minus the command, so that it
reports the controller's own landing offset, not the IFM's correction.
All of these can be estimated on a real robot. Encoders $f_s$, $f_h$,
and $f_c$ embed the state, the history, and each candidate, and each
choice becomes a token $\mathbf t_i$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf z = f_s(\mathbf x_g) + W_h\, f_h(\mathbf h), \qquad
  \mathbf t_i = \mathrm{ReLU}\big(W_t [\mathbf z; f_c(\mathbf q_i)]\big),
  \label{eq:context}
\end{equation}
with a learned embedding in place of $f_c(\mathbf q_i)$ for the no-step
option.

\looseness=-1 The candidate mixer, a small pre-norm transformer encoder
\citep{vaswani_attention_2017}, lets the tokens attend to each other,
so each choice is judged against the alternatives. No token attends to
empty slots. A shared network $g$ and two linear heads give each
choice a score and a swing time,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_i = \mathbf w_\alpha^\top g(\tilde{\mathbf t}_i), \qquad
  T_i = T_{\min} + (T_{\max} - T_{\min})\,
    \operatorname{sig}\big(\mathbf w_T^\top g(\tilde{\mathbf t}_i)\big),
  \label{eq:heads}
\end{equation}
with $\tilde{\mathbf t}_i$ the mixed token and
$\operatorname{sig}$ the logistic function. A softmax of the scores
over the valid choices is the policy. In training, GaitNet samples
a choice and an unclipped swing time from a Gaussian around the predicted one, and at deployment it takes the top-scoring choice
and its predicted swing time. The projection $W_h$, the attention output projections, and the
last layer of each feed-forward block start at zero, so GaitNet begins
as exactly the per-candidate network of \citet{Sullivan2025}, which
scores each candidate alone from the state and the candidate code.

\subsection{Inverse Footstep Model}
\label{subsec:method-idm}

\looseness=-1 Our training controller keeps each swing's target fixed
relative to the hip, which moves forward, so the foot
lands past the commanded point, more so at higher speed, where the
overshoot exceeds the candidate margin (App.~\ref{app:settings}). OCS2 instead fixes the target in the world at
command time. For each controller, the layer sends
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf u = I^c(\zeta, \mathbf u^\star),
  \qquad\text{so that}\qquad
  F_c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u) \approx \mathbf u^\star .
  \label{eq:idm}
\end{equation}
We log the steps that the controller executes as tuples $(\zeta, \mathbf u,
\mathbf o)$. Whatever foothold a command was meant
to reach, it landed the foot at $\mathbf o$, so in hindsight every logged
step is an example of the inverse map from landing to command
\citep{jordan_forward_1992, andrychowicz_hindsight_2017}, as in
\citet{akizhanov_learning_2024}. We therefore fit the IFM with the landing
as input and the command as target, since the
desired foothold takes the landing's place at
deployment,
\begin{equation}
  \min_\theta \; \mathbb E\,
  \big\| \mathbf u - I^c(\zeta, \mathbf o) \big\|^2,
  \qquad
  I^c(\zeta, \mathbf o) = \mathbf o + \Delta_\theta(\zeta, \mathbf o),
  \label{eq:idm-loss}
\end{equation}
\looseness=-1 where $\Delta_\theta$ is a small multilayer perceptron whose last
layer starts at zero, so an untrained IFM sends GaitNet's choice
unchanged. Fitting the inverse directly gives the command in one forward
pass, with no search over a learned forward model. For the
training controller, we log steps with Gaussian command noise. For OCS2,
each seed's frozen GaitNet logs fewer than 20{,}000 steps with
the training controller's IFM, on worlds separate from the evaluation,
and gets its own IFM (App.~\ref{app:settings}).

\subsection{What the Inverse Footstep Model Can Correct}
\label{subsec:idm-theory}

\looseness=-1 The IFM sees $\zeta$ but not the full state $\mathbf s$, so
given $\zeta$ and the command the landing is random even though $F_c$ is
deterministic. We split the landing into its conditional mean and a
zero-mean residual $\boldsymbol\eta$, and the \emph{landing error} of
the IFM's command into two parts:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathbf o &= \bar F_c(\zeta,\mathbf u) + \boldsymbol\eta, &
  \bar F_c(\zeta,\mathbf u) &:= \mathbb E[\mathbf o \mid \zeta,\mathbf u],\\
  \mathbf e &:= \mathbf o - \mathbf u^\star = \boldsymbol\beta + \boldsymbol\eta, &
  \boldsymbol\beta &:= \bar F_c(\zeta,\mathbf u) - \mathbf u^\star .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mean-forward}
\end{equation}
The IFM acts only through $\boldsymbol\beta$, the part of the error that
its inputs predict, and a better fit shrinks only its fitting part; no
IFM with these inputs removes $\boldsymbol\eta$. The regression of Eq.~\eqref{eq:idm-loss} cancels
$\boldsymbol\beta$ if the landing law is the same when logging and at
deployment, the logged landings carry no residual, each landing is
reached by a convex set of commands, the logged landings cover the
desired footholds, and the fit is exact
(Proposition~\ref{prop:idm-mean} in App.~\ref{app:idm-proofs}). Neither
deployed IFM meets all of these, since the training controller's IFM is
fitted without the controller randomization of training and the OCS2 IFM
on the footholds that GaitNet reached through the training controller's
IFM, so Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-why} tests the refit beyond the proven
case. With a
residual, even an exact fit is biased: in a linear-Gaussian model, it
pulls the landing toward the mean logged landing by an amount linear in
the foothold's offset from that mean (Proposition~\ref{prop:idm-shrink}),
the attenuation bias of a regression on a noisy input
\citep{fuller_measurement_1987, soderstrom_errors_2007}, largest for
footholds the logging policy seldom reached.

\looseness=-1 Let the \emph{clearance} $d(\mathbf u^\star) :=
\inf_{\mathbf p \in \mathcal V} \|\mathbf p - \mathbf u^\star\|_\infty$
be the foothold's distance to the nearest point of the voids $\mathcal
V$. Since $\|\mathbf e\|_\infty \le \|\boldsymbol\beta\| +
\|\boldsymbol\eta\|$, with $\|\cdot\|$ Euclidean, a point foot cannot
land in a void if this sum is below $d(\mathbf u^\star)$ (App.~\ref{app:idm-void}), so the candidate margin must
cover both the error the IFM leaves and the residual. With exact
terrain and a point foot, the mask of Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-step} gives
every candidate a clearance of at least $m$ for void edges parallel to the grid axes and
at least $m - h/2$ at any angle (Lemma~\ref{lem:idm-grid}), for square
voids at least $2\sqrt2\,h$ wide, as all our tiles are. The foot's
radius and perception and odometry errors reduce this clearance; on
OCS2, the depth map's own distance to unwalkable cells (App.~\ref{app:deployment}) adds to it. On
flat ground, the refitted IFM cut OCS2's RMS landing error from three
times the margin to about the margin (Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-why}).

\subsection{Training}
\label{subsec:method-training}

\looseness=-1 We train GaitNet with PPO
\citep{Schulman_proximal_2017} in Isaac Lab
\citep{mittal_orbit_2023}, with many robots in parallel.
The robot follows a commanded forward velocity over terrain in which a
fraction of the tiles, the \emph{void fraction}, are deep voids, and a
curriculum raises it. The reward per tick is
\begin{equation}
  r = \Delta t\,\Big[\,w_a\,\mathbf 1_{\text{alive}}
  + e^{-\|\mathbf v^{usr}_{xy} - \mathbf v_{b,xy}\|^2/\sigma_v^2}
  + e^{-(\omega^{usr}_z - \omega_{b,z})^2/\sigma_\omega^2}
  - w_s\,\mathbf 1_{\text{step}}
  - w_f\,\mathbf 1_{\text{fail}}\Big],
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with commanded ($usr$) and base ($b$) velocities, and no gait,
foot-clearance, posture, or smoothness terms. The training controller's
IFM sits frozen in the loop from the first iteration and corrects only
GaitNet's chosen candidate. At every reset, each
robot's controller gets new tracking quality, swing height, landing bias and noise, and lift-off delay
(App.~\ref{app:training}), a randomization absent from the IFM's data.
PPO's critic reads the state and candidates only, without attention
(App.~\ref{app:training}).

\subsection{Deployment on OCS2}
\label{subsec:method-deployment}

\looseness=-1 On OCS2, GaitNet replaces the gait scheduler of the
\texttt{legged\_control} stack \citep{leggedcontrol,
grandia_perceptive_2022} in Gazebo, where OCS2's NMPC plans the motion
and a weighted WBC tracks it, with all OCS2 settings stock. Its candidates
are built as in training, with the same margin, and the OCS2 IFM corrects
its chosen candidate. The NMPC receives the corrected command as a
foot-placement constraint: the command is projected onto the nearest
planar region of the segmented terrain map, and the foot must land in a
box of $\pm$\corlAuthorityCm\,cm around that point inside the region,
while the swing planner receives only touchdown heights. Each step joins the steps already planned in OCS2's
contact schedule and starts at the tick it is chosen. Terrain comes
from a simulated RealSense D435 depth camera and odometry from OCS2's
leg-based state estimator (App.~\ref{app:deployment}).
