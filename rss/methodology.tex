\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

GaitNet chooses at every tick which step to take, if any; an execution
layer turns that step into a command and a start time that the
controller in use lands where and when GaitNet meant
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Settings are listed in
App.~\ref{app:settings}.

\subsection{Choosing One Step per Tick}
\label{subsec:method-step}

At every tick GaitNet may start at most one step for one leg, while the
other legs keep their current commands. Because a swing lasts several
ticks, GaitNet may start a second swing before the first one ends; the
feasibility rules of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} decide which legs may step,
where a leg that is waiting to lift off counts as lifted. Beyond these
rules, GaitNet never chooses a gait phase, leg pair or contact sequence.
It commits only to the next step, and nothing in its action
space ties its steps to a cycle: at each tick it asks whether any
available step is better than doing nothing.

The candidates are safe cells near each hip. A grid of terrain rays at
\corlScanResCm\,cm spacing around the hip marks a cell as safe when the
ground under it is within reach below the hip, and as unsafe when it is
a void or the ray misses. The unsafe cells are grown by
\numberstringnum{\corlMarginCm} cells, so that no unsafe ray lies
within $m = \corlMarginCm$\,cm of a remaining cell along the grid axes,
and, with exact terrain, the void is at least 1.5\,cm away at any edge orientation
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:idm-theory}); no other
reach, edge or kinematic rule is applied. For each leg that may step,
GaitNet draws \corlTrainCandidatesPerLeg{} safe cells at random without
repeats and masks empty slots when a leg has fewer. With the no-step
option, these form the choices of a tick, built the same way in
training and at deployment, apart from the margin.

\subsection{Network}
\label{subsec:method-network}

GaitNet has three inputs: the tracking state $\mathbf x_g$ (the feet's
planar positions relative to the trunk, base height, base linear and
angular velocity, the velocity command, the controller's scheduled
contacts and projected gravity, in the trunk frame except the height);
the candidates, each described by $\mathbf q_i$, a one-hot code of its
leg and its offset from the hip; and the history $\mathbf h$ of the last
\corlHistSeconds\,s (\corlHistTicks{} ticks) of base velocities, foot
positions relative to the hips from the joint encoders, scheduled
contacts, and each leg's last touchdown error (the foot's position at
its scheduled touchdown minus the command). All can be estimated on a
real robot. A state encoder $f_s$ and a history encoder $f_h$ form a context
shared by all choices, a candidate encoder embeds each candidate, and
each choice becomes a token,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf z = f_s(\mathbf x_g) + W_h\, f_h(\mathbf h), \qquad
  \mathbf t_i = \mathrm{ReLU}\big(W_t [\mathbf z; f_c(\mathbf q_i)]\big),
  \label{eq:context}
\end{equation}
with a learned embedding in place of $f_c(\mathbf q_i)$ for the no-step
option $\varnothing$. So that a choice can be judged against the
alternatives, a candidate mixer, a small pre-norm transformer encoder
\citep{vaswani_attention_2017}, lets the tokens attend to each other;
empty slots are masked as keys. A readout $g$ and two linear heads give
each choice a score and a swing time,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_i = \mathbf w_\alpha^\top g(\tilde{\mathbf t}_i), \qquad
  T_i = T_{\min} + (T_{\max} - T_{\min})\,
    \sigma\big(\mathbf w_T^\top g(\tilde{\mathbf t}_i)\big),
  \label{eq:heads}
\end{equation}
with $\tilde{\mathbf t}_i$ the mixed token and $\sigma$ the logistic
function. The scores of the valid choices are the logits of a
categorical distribution $\pi(i) \propto \exp(\alpha_i)$. In training,
GaitNet samples a choice and a swing time $T \sim \mathcal N(T_i,
\sigma_T^2)$; at deployment it takes the top-scoring choice and $T_i$.
$W_h$, the attention output projections and the last layer of each
feed-forward block start at zero, so at initialization GaitNet computes
exactly the per-candidate network of \citet{Sullivan2025}, which scores
each candidate on its own from $\mathbf x_g$ and $\mathbf q_i$; as in
that network, $\mathbf q_i$ carries a last entry for a separate cost,
which we keep at zero. During training GaitNet learns to use the history
and to compare candidates.

\subsection{Inverse Footstep Model}
\label{subsec:method-idm}

Our training controller keeps re-aiming each swing at the moving hip, so
the foot lands past the commanded point, the more so the faster the
robot walks: by +\corlOvershootSlowMin{} to +\corlOvershootSlowMax\,cm
along the walking direction at \corlSimSpeedSlow\,m/s and
+\corlOvershootFastMin{} to +\corlOvershootFastMax\,cm at
\corlSimSpeedFast\,m/s, against the \corlMarginCm\,cm margin. OCS2, in
contrast, fixes the target in the world when the step is commanded. Every
candidate, including the chosen $\mathbf u^\star$, already lies in a
cell that the mask marks as safe, so we treat $\mathbf u^\star$ as the
desired landing and, for controller $c$, send
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf u = I^c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u^\star),
  \qquad\text{so that}\qquad
  F_c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u) \approx \mathbf u^\star .
  \label{eq:idm}
\end{equation}
GaitNet is the same for every controller; only $I^c$ changes. The IDM is
a small MLP that predicts a correction,
\begin{equation}
  I^c_\theta(\mathbf h_{\text{last}}, \ell, T, \mathbf o) =
  \mathbf o + \Delta^c_\theta(\mathbf h_{\text{last}}, \ell, T, \mathbf o),
  \label{eq:idm-model}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf h_{\text{last}}$, the newest tick of GaitNet's history,
stands in for $\mathbf s$; its last layer starts at zero, so an untrained
IDM sends GaitNet's choice unchanged. We log steps that $c$ executes as
tuples $(\mathbf h_{\text{last}}, \ell, T, \mathbf u, \mathbf o)$ and fit
\begin{equation}
  \min_\theta \; \mathbb E\,
  \big\| \mathbf u - I^c_\theta(\mathbf h_{\text{last}}, \ell, T, \mathbf o) \big\|^2 .
  \label{eq:idm-loss}
\end{equation}
Whatever $\mathbf u$ was aimed at, it did land the foot at $\mathbf o$,
so $(\mathbf o, \mathbf u)$ is an example of the inverse map
\citep{jordan_forward_1992, andrychowicz_hindsight_2017}; the same
construction corrects NMPC contact targets in
\citet{akizhanov_learning_2024}. For the training controller, the steps
come from two earlier policies without controller randomization, at up
to 30\% voids and across our speed range, with Gaussian noise added to
every command to widen the data around the footholds a policy prefers.
For OCS2, we log \verify{fewer than 20{,}000 steps of the frozen GaitNet
run on OCS2 with the training controller's IDM}, on worlds kept separate
from those of the evaluation \verify{(OCS2-IDM fit details and held-out error
to be stated)}.

\subsection{What the Inverse Footstep Model Estimates}
\label{subsec:idm-theory}

Let $\zeta := (\mathbf h_{\text{last}}, \ell, T)$ be the IDM's inputs
besides the landing, write $I^c(\zeta,\cdot)$ for the fitted IDM
$I^c_\theta$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:idm-model}, and let $P_c$ be the
distribution of the logged tuples. Norms are Euclidean unless marked.
Since $\zeta$ omits part of $\mathbf s$, the landing is random given
$(\zeta,\mathbf u)$ even if $F_c$ is deterministic:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf o = \bar F_c(\zeta,\mathbf u) + \boldsymbol\eta, \qquad
  \bar F_c(\zeta,\mathbf u) := \mathbb E[\mathbf o \mid \zeta,\mathbf u].
  \label{eq:mean-forward}
\end{equation}
We assume this conditional law is the same when logging and at
deployment, although both policies choose commands from terrain that
$\zeta$ omits; it fails for the training controller's IDM, which runs
under a randomization it was not fitted on
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}). Proofs are in
App.~\ref{app:idm-proofs}.

\begin{assumption}[Exact relabelling]
\label{as:idm-exact}
(i)~$\boldsymbol\eta = \mathbf 0$ almost surely under $P_c$;
(ii)~for every $(\zeta,\mathbf o)$, the logged commands that land at
$\mathbf o$ form a convex set (at most one point if $\mathbf u \mapsto
\bar F_c(\zeta,\mathbf u) - \mathbf u$ is Lipschitz with constant below
one); (iii)~every deployment pair $(\zeta,\mathbf u^\star)$ lies in the
support of the logged $(\zeta,\mathbf o)$, and $\bar F_c$ and
$\mathbb E_{P_c}[\mathbf u \mid \zeta,\mathbf o]$ have continuous
versions; (iv)~$I^c$ is continuous and equals
$I^c_\star := \mathbb E_{P_c}[\mathbf u \mid \zeta,\mathbf o]$
$P_c$-almost everywhere.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Hindsight regression]
\label{prop:idm-mean}
If $\mathbb E_{P_c}\|\mathbf u\|^2 < \infty$, then $I^c_\star$
minimises the loss in Eq.~\eqref{eq:idm-loss} over all square-integrable
functions of $(\zeta,\mathbf o)$, uniquely up to $P_c$-null sets. Under
Assumption~\ref{as:idm-exact}, $\bar F_c(\zeta, I^c(\zeta,\mathbf
u^\star)) = \mathbf u^\star$ for every deployment pair.
\end{proposition}

Each logged command did land the foot at $\mathbf o$ from its state, so
noise added to a command before it is sent does not corrupt its label.
When Assumption~\ref{as:idm-exact} fails, Eq.~\eqref{eq:idm-loss}
averages $\mathbf u$ over the hidden states and noise that share
$(\zeta,\mathbf o)$: without (ii), commands from two inverse branches
can average to one that lands at neither \citep{jordan_forward_1992};
without (iii), the held-out error says nothing off the logged support;
and without (i), the regression is in general biased even with
unlimited data \citep{eysenbach_imitating_2022}.

\begin{proposition}[Relabelling bias]
\label{prop:idm-shrink}
Fix $\zeta$. Let $\mathbf u \sim \mathcal N(\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma_u)$ and
$\mathbf o = A\mathbf u + \mathbf b + \boldsymbol\eta$ with
$\boldsymbol\eta \sim \mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\Sigma_\eta)$ independent of
$\mathbf u$, $\Sigma_u \succ 0$ and $A$ invertible, and let
$\bar{\mathbf o} = A\boldsymbol\mu + \mathbf b$ and $S = A\Sigma_u
A^\top + \Sigma_\eta$. The landing $\mathbf o^\star := A\,I^c_\star(\zeta,
\mathbf u^\star) + \mathbf b + \boldsymbol\eta$ of the deployed command
has the mean error
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb E[\mathbf o^\star] - \mathbf u^\star
  = -\Lambda\,(\mathbf u^\star - \bar{\mathbf o}), \qquad
  \Lambda = \Sigma_\eta S^{-1},
  \label{eq:idm-shrink}
\end{equation}
where the eigenvalues of $\Lambda$ lie in $[0,1)$ and
$\|\mathbb E[\mathbf o^\star] - \bar{\mathbf o}\|_{S^{-1}} \le
\|\mathbf u^\star - \bar{\mathbf o}\|_{S^{-1}}$, with
$\|\mathbf x\|_{S^{-1}}^2 := \mathbf x^\top S^{-1}\mathbf x$.
\end{proposition}

The landing is pulled toward the mean logged landing $\bar{\mathbf o}$,
a contraction in the Mahalanobis metric of the logged landings, by a
bias linear in $\mathbf u^\star - \bar{\mathbf o}$: along any direction
it grows with the target's distance from $\bar{\mathbf o}$, so it is
largest for footholds the logging policy seldom reached. This is the
attenuation of a regression on a noisy regressor. Spreading the logged
commands (larger $\Sigma_u$) does not raise any eigenvalue of
$\Lambda$, although it need not shrink the bias at every target; this
is a second reason, besides coverage, for the command noise in the
training controller's logs.

Let $\mathcal V$ be the void points, as offsets from the hip in the yaw
frame of the decision, and $d(\mathbf u^\star) := \inf_{\mathbf p \in
\mathcal V} \|\mathbf p - \mathbf u^\star\|_\infty$ along the ray grid.
With exact rays at spacing $h = \corlScanResCm$\,cm, unsafe cells grown
by $m/h$ cells, and voids made of squares of side at least
$3\sqrt2\,h \approx 4.2$\,cm (our terrain cells are
\corlTerrainCellCm\,cm in training and \corlGzTileCm\,cm in Gazebo) whose
edges make an angle $\varphi$ with the rays, every candidate has
$d(\mathbf u^\star) \ge (\corlMarginCm - \tfrac12|\sin 2\varphi|)$\,cm
(Lemma~\ref{lem:idm-grid}): $\corlMarginCm$\,cm for edges parallel to
the rays, 1.5\,cm at $45^\circ$, before perception and odometry errors
and the foot's radius.

\begin{proposition}[Landing error and voids]
\label{prop:idm-void}
Send $\mathbf u = I^c(\zeta,\mathbf u^\star)$, let $\mathcal G$ be the
information at the decision, which fixes $\zeta$, $\mathbf u^\star$,
$\mathbf u$ and $\mathcal V$, and let $\boldsymbol\beta := \bar
F_c(\zeta,\mathbf u) - \mathbf u^\star$. Then $\mathbf e := \mathbf o -
\mathbf u^\star = \boldsymbol\beta + \boldsymbol\eta$, and
(a)~if $\|\boldsymbol\beta\| + \|\boldsymbol\eta\| < d(\mathbf
u^\star)$, the foot does not land in a void;
(b)~$\Pr(\mathbf o \in \mathcal V \mid \mathcal G) \le
\Pr(\|\mathbf e\|_\infty \ge d(\mathbf u^\star) \mid \mathcal G)$;
(c)~if $\boldsymbol\eta \sim \mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\sigma_\eta^2
\mathbf I_2)$ given $\mathcal G$, then $\Pr(\mathbf o \in \mathcal V
\mid \mathcal G) \le \exp\!\big(-(d(\mathbf u^\star) -
\|\boldsymbol\beta\|)_+^2 / (2\sigma_\eta^2)\big)$, and four times this
if instead, given $\mathcal G$, its components along the grid axes are
zero-mean and $\sigma_\eta$-sub-Gaussian.
\end{proposition}

The IDM acts only through $\boldsymbol\beta$, the part of the landing
error that $\zeta$ predicts, not through $\boldsymbol\eta$. If
$\bar F_c(\zeta,\cdot)$ is $L$-Lipschitz, $\|\boldsymbol\beta\|$ is at
most $L$ times the IDM's fitting error at the target plus the
relabelling bias $\|\bar F_c(\zeta, I^c_\star(\zeta,\mathbf u^\star)) -
\mathbf u^\star\|$: zero under Assumption~\ref{as:idm-exact},
$\|\Lambda(\mathbf u^\star - \bar{\mathbf o})\|$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:idm-shrink} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-beta}). Hypothesis
(c) also needs the nearby voids to neither shift nor widen the error.

\begin{remark}[Landing error against the margin]
\label{rem:idm-numbers}
On flat ground, the IDM cuts OCS2's landing error from
\corlOcsLandRmsNoIdm\ to \corlOcsLandRmsIdm\,cm RMS
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-why}). For zero-mean isotropic Gaussian
$\mathbf e$ with per-axis standard deviation $\sigma$, a foothold at
distance $d$ from a long straight void edge enters the void with
probability $\Phi(-d/\sigma)$, $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution
function (App.~\ref{app:idm-proofs}): at $d = 2$\,cm, $0.08$ if $2$\,cm
is the RMS of $\|\mathbf e\|$ and $0.16$ if it is per axis
\verify{(convention to be confirmed)}, so ten such footholds among the
roughly 97 of a trial (\corlOcsStepsPerMFull{} steps/m over
\corlOcsTrackM\,m) would leave the trial void-free with probability
only $0.44$ or $0.18$. OCS2's \verify{\corlGzProbeMarginCm}\,cm margin
lowers the per-foothold values to $0.02$ or $0.07$; we have not
measured how close GaitNet's footholds come to voids.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Training}
\label{subsec:method-training}

We train GaitNet with PPO \citep{Schulman_proximal_2017} in Isaac Lab
\citep{mittal_orbit_2023}, with \corlParallelEnvs{} robots in parallel.
The evaluated checkpoint was trained from scratch for about
\verify{\corlOcsCheckpoint} iterations, about \corlTrainHours\,h on one
NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU. The critic is a separate, smaller
network that reads the state and candidates but not the history. The
training controller's IDM is fitted first, offline, and then sits fixed
between GaitNet and the controller from the first iteration; GaitNet
still sees the raw candidates. Each robot's controller is redrawn at
every reset (tracking quality, swing height, landing bias and noise,
lift-off delay; App.~\ref{app:training}), although the IDM was fitted
without this randomization. The robot follows a forward velocity
command over terrain in which a fraction of the cells, the void
fraction, are deep voids, and a curriculum raises the void fraction.
The reward per tick is
\begin{equation}
  r = \Delta t\,\Big[\,w_a\,\mathbb{I}_{\text{alive}}
  + e^{-\|\mathbf v^{usr}_{xy} - \mathbf v_{b,xy}\|^2/\sigma_v^2}
  + e^{-(\omega^{usr}_z - \omega_{b,z})^2/\sigma_\omega^2}
  - w_s\,\mathbb{I}_{\text{step}}
  - w_f\,\mathbb{I}_{\text{fail}}\Big],
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with commanded ($usr$) and base ($b$) velocities, and no gait,
clearance, posture or smoothness terms (failure conditions in
App.~\ref{app:training}).

\subsection{Deployment on OCS2}
\label{subsec:method-deployment}

On OCS2, GaitNet replaces the gait scheduler of the
\texttt{legged\_control} stack \citep{leggedcontrol,
grandia_perceptive_2022} in Gazebo, where OCS2's NMPC plans the motion
and a weighted whole-body controller tracks it
\verify{with stock task settings}. GaitNet is queried every tick, also
while two legs swing, with candidates built as in training but with a margin of
\verify{\corlGzProbeMarginCm}\,cm and the IDM fitted on OCS2's own steps. The IDM
fixes where the foot lands; when it lands is set by how steps are
delivered: each step is written into OCS2's contact schedule at absolute
times on the NMPC clock, a fixed lead of \corlGzLead\,s after the
decision, and merged with the steps already planned. Terrain comes from
a simulated RealSense D435 depth camera through a depth-vote map, and
odometry from OCS2's leg-based state estimator with a slip gate
(App.~\ref{app:deployment}).
