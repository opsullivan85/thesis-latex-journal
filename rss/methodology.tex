\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

% CADENCE
% has three parts (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Its core %, \emph{GaitNet},
% scores the footstep options of a tick one at a
% time, each from the robot's state and that option alone, next to a
% no-step option, and predicts a swing time. One option is taken per
% tick, with up to two legs in swing. CADENCE adds a candidate
% mixer and a history, which compare the options with each other and with
% the recent motion (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}), and an IFM, with
% which it moves to a second controller (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}
% and~\ref{subsec:method-training}).

% CADENCE (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview})
% takes in individual footstep options alongside robot's current and historical state, and mixes candidate 
% tokens, resulting in a score and proposed swing time for each candidate footstep option.
% The selected footstep option is then run through an IFM, with
% which it moves to a second controller (Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}
% and~\ref{subsec:method-training}).

CADENCE (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}) turns each candidate footstep, and a
no-step option, into a token from that option and the robot's current
state and recent history; a candidate mixer compares the tokens with each
other, and each gets a score and a swing time
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}). One option is taken per tick, with
up to two legs in swing. The chosen footstep is sent through the
controller's IFM, and swapping the IFM moves CADENCE to a second
controller without retraining the underlying footstep planning
(Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-idm} and~\ref{subsec:method-training}).

\subsection{A Small Action Space}
\label{subsec:method-step}

% \looseness=-1 At each tick, CADENCE takes one of at most
% \corlTotalCandidates{} discrete choices, the no-step option or one of
% \corlTrainCandidatesPerLeg{} candidate footholds for each of the
% \corlNumLegs{} legs (Fig.~\ref{fig:action}), and predicts a swing time
% for the chosen step. The action holds no joint target, continuous
% foothold, gait parameter, or phase, so the controller still plans and
% tracks the motion, and the timing, order, overlap, and pairing of steps
% follow only from which choice wins each tick. %Apart from the contact
% %rule, the per-step cost $w_s$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:reward}, and one
% %candidate per leg pinned near a regular stride's foothold, which sets
% %where but not when a leg steps. 
% CADENCE's gait timing and order are emergent.
% It's candidate footsteps are chosen from nearby steppable terrain, which we
% sample uniformly from safe locations in a 1cm grid with 2cm of clearance added around voids.


\looseness=-1 At each tick, CADENCE takes one of at most
\corlTotalCandidates{} discrete choices, the no-step option, or one of
\corlTrainCandidatesPerLeg{} candidate footholds for each of the
\corlNumLegs{} legs (Fig.~\ref{fig:action}). 
The gait, timing, and order are emergent properties of CADENCE's action
space and sampling rate. The foothold choices contain desired
swing durations, but not joint targets, or gait parameters. The
model-based controller has full control over the swing within the position
and timing constraints set by CADENCE.
The candidate footsteps are chosen from nearby steppable terrain, which we
sample uniformly from safe locations in a 1cm grid with 2cm of clearance added around voids.

\subsection{Network}
\label{subsec:method-network}

\looseness=-1 CADENCE embeds the robot state $\mathbf x_g$, a
\corlHistSeconds\,s history $\mathbf h$ of base velocities, foot
positions, scheduled contacts, and each leg's last \emph{touchdown
error} (its landing minus the command sent), and each candidate's code
$\mathbf q_i$ (its leg and offset from the hip) into one token per
choice (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}a),
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf z = f_s(\mathbf x_g) + W_h\, f_h(\mathbf h), \qquad
  \mathbf t_i = \mathrm{ReLU}\big(W_t [\mathbf z; f_c(\mathbf q_i)]\big),
  \label{eq:context}
\end{equation}
with a learned embedding for the no-step option. The \emph{candidate
mixer}, a small transformer encoder \citep{vaswani_attention_2017},
lets the tokens attend to each other, so every choice, waiting
included, is judged against the alternatives. A shared network $g$ then
gives each mixed token $\tilde{\mathbf t}_i$ a score and a swing time,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_i = \mathbf w_\alpha^\top g(\tilde{\mathbf t}_i), \qquad
  T_i = T_{\min} + (T_{\max} - T_{\min})\,
    \operatorname{sig}\big(\mathbf w_T^\top g(\tilde{\mathbf t}_i)\big),
  \label{eq:heads}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sig}$ is the logistic function, and the policy is
a softmax of the scores, sampled in training and maximized at
deployment. With $W_h$ and the mixer's output layers
% initialized at zero, CADENCE starts as GaitNet, a \emph{per-candidate network},
% which scores each candidate alone from the state and its code
% (App.~\ref{app:settings}).
initialized at zero, CADENCE starts out ranking each candidate in isolation and only introduces mixing as it learns to (App.~\ref{app:settings}).

\subsection{Inverse Footstep Model}
\label{subsec:method-idm}

\looseness=-1 Our training controller keeps each swing's target fixed
relative to the moving hip, so the foot lands past the command, by up to
\corlOvershootFastMax\,cm at \corlSimSpeedFast\,m/s, about twice the
candidate margin, whereas OCS2 fixes the target in the world. Their footstep
maps $F_c$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:forward}) therefore differ, and for each
controller the IFM sends a command $\mathbf u = I^c(\zeta,\mathbf
u^\star)$ meant to satisfy $F_c(\mathbf s,\ell,T,\mathbf u) \approx
\mathbf u^\star$. Every logged step $(\zeta,\mathbf u,\mathbf o)$ shows,
in hindsight, a command that landed the foot at $\mathbf o$, so we fit
the inverse directly (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}b), with the landing as input
\citep{jordan_forward_1992, andrychowicz_hindsight_2017,
akizhanov_learning_2024}:
\begin{equation}
  \min_\theta \; \mathbb E\,
  \big\| \mathbf u - I^c(\zeta, \mathbf o) \big\|^2,
  \qquad
  I^c(\zeta, \mathbf o) = \mathbf o + \Delta_\theta(\zeta, \mathbf o),
  \label{eq:idm-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_\theta$ is a small multilayer perceptron whose output
layer is initialized at zero, so an unfitted IFM is the identity. For
OCS2, each training seed's frozen CADENCE logs
fewer than 20{,}000 steps through the training controller's IFM, on
worlds separate from the evaluation, and its own OCS2 IFM is fitted on
them.
\phantomsection\label{subsec:idm-theory}Since the IFM does not see the
full state, it can cancel only the part of the landing error that its
inputs predict, and is guaranteed to cancel it only under conditions
that neither deployed IFM meets (App.~\ref{app:idm-proofs}); the
experiments therefore carry the transfer claim
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-why}).

\subsection{Training and Deployment}
\label{subsec:method-training}

\looseness=-1 We train CADENCE with PPO \citep{Schulman_proximal_2017}
in Isaac Lab \citep{mittal_orbit_2023}, on terrain whose void fraction
a curriculum raises to \corlMaxVoidPct\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:training-terrain-combined}), with the reward
\begin{equation}
  r = \Delta t\,\Big[\,w_a\,\mathbf 1_{\text{alive}}
  + e^{-\|\mathbf v^{\mathrm{usr}}_{xy} - \mathbf v_{b,xy}\|^2/\sigma_v^2}
  + e^{-(\omega^{\mathrm{usr}}_z - \omega_{b,z})^2/\sigma_\omega^2}
  - w_s\,\mathbf 1_{\text{step}}
  - w_f\,\mathbf 1_{\text{fail}}\Big],
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
which tracks the commanded (usr) base ($b$) velocities and charges
each step and failure but has no gait, clearance, or posture term
(weights $w_a, w_s, w_f$ and widths $\sigma_v, \sigma_\omega$ in
App.~\ref{app:settings}). The training controller's IFM is frozen in the loop (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}c), and the controller is
randomized at every reset (App.~\ref{app:training}).

\phantomsection\label{subsec:method-deployment}\looseness=-1 On OCS2
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}d), CADENCE replaces the gait scheduler of the \texttt{legged\_control}
stack \citep{leggedcontrol, grandia_perceptive_2022} in Gazebo, with
