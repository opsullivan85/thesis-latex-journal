\noindent\textbf{Gaits in model-based control.} \looseness=-1
Model-based controllers usually take their contact timing from a cyclic
gait \citep{dicarlo_dynamic_2018, grandia_perceptive_2022}, while
planners optimize timing or footholds \citep{winkler_gait_2018,
jenelten_tamols_2022}. Gait-free planners choose contacts online by
kinematic rules \citep{bjelonic_whole-body_2021} or tree search
\citep{amatucci_monte_2022, taouil_non-gaited_2024}, or let a learned
agent set an MPC's contact timing, from which acyclic gaits also emerge
\citep{patrizi_rl-augmented_2026}. However, the tree searches rate
contact sequences by the controller's own optimal cost, and the learned
agent chooses no footholds and is trained for the MPC it runs with,
whereas CADENCE sends an unchanged controller one step at a time,
foothold included, and moves between controllers.

\noindent\textbf{Learned gaits and footholds.} \looseness=-1 End-to-end
policies decide contacts implicitly, in a large action space of joint
or foot targets \citep{lee_learning_2020, zhang_learning_2023}. Hybrids
learn the parameters of a cyclic gait or per-foot contact states with
heuristic footholds \citep{yang_fast_2021, margolis_learning_2021,
da_learning_2020, yang_cajun_2023}, or footholds under a fixed gait
\citep{gangapurwala_rloc_2022, villarreal_fast_2019}. Close to CADENCE
in its action space is ContactNet \citep{bratta_contactnet_2024}, a
supervised network that ranks discrete footholds near each hip and so
chooses which legs step and where, in an acyclic order. However, its
walk or trot setting presets the swing times and how many legs swing,
and it replans only after each touchdown.

\noindent\textbf{Transfer between controllers.} \looseness=-1 Policies
move to a new system through an inverse dynamics model fitted on its
data \citep{christiano_transfer_2016, xie_fada_2026}, a grounded
simulator \citep{hanna_grounded_2017}, or online adaptation
\citep{kumar_rma_2021}. \citet{akizhanov_learning_2024} correct an
NMPC's contact targets with a network fitted on the contacts it reached,
within a fixed gait and on one controller. The IFM is classic direct
inverse modelling \citep{jordan_forward_1992} with hindsight relabelling
\citep{andrychowicz_hindsight_2017}. To our knowledge, no prior work
moves a gait-free footstep planner between two model-based controllers
by refitting only an IFM.
