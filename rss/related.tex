\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% 2026-11-04 flow rewrite: plainer words, less repetition; same facts, numbers and \verify items.

\noindent\textbf{Contact and gait planning.} \looseness=-1 Model-based
planners shrink the contact search to convex foothold regions
\citep{deits_footstep_2014, grandia_perceptive_2022} or optimize
schedules and timing together \citep{winkler_gait_2018}, or footholds and
base motion over the terrain \citep{jenelten_tamols_2022}.
Gait-free planners choose contact timing online, from per-leg
feasibility rules \citep{bjelonic_whole-body_2021} or by tree search
\citep{amatucci_monte_2022} sped up by a learned cost-to-go
\citep{taouil_non-gaited_2024}. \citet{patrizi_rl-augmented_2026}
learn, inside an MPC, when each foot is in the air. These
planners search contacts inside the controller's optimization; GaitNet
leaves the controller unchanged.

\noindent\textbf{Learned gaits and footholds.} \looseness=-1 End-to-end
policies \citep{lee_learning_2020, zhang_learning_2023,
fu_minimizing_2021} decide contacts implicitly, in joint targets that a
model-based controller cannot use. Hybrid methods learn
part of a model-based controller's gait: gait parameters
\citep{yang_fast_2021}, contact primitives \citep{da_learning_2020},
per-foot contact states on gap terrain \citep{margolis_learning_2021},
or stepping frequency with swing-foot residuals \citep{yang_cajun_2023}.
Their footholds come from heuristics, at most with learned residuals.
Others learn footholds while a fixed gait sets the timing
\citep{gangapurwala_rloc_2022, yu_visual-locomotion_2021,
villarreal_fast_2019}. DeepGait
\citep{tsounis_deepgait_2020} also chooses the legs in contact, but for a
learned controller trained with it.
ContactNet \citep{bratta_contactnet_2024} ranks foothold
regions without prescribing the swing leg, but its configuration and
gait set how many legs swing at once and when, and it re-ranks
only at touchdown. Closest is the earlier GaitNet of \citet{Sullivan2025},
which scores each safe cell alone and was run only on its training
controller.

\noindent\textbf{Transfer between controllers.} \looseness=-1 Prior work
transfers a fixed learned policy through an inverse dynamics model
fitted or adapted on the target system \citep{christiano_transfer_2016,
xie_fada_2026}, by grounding the simulator in target data
\citep{hanna_grounded_2017}, or through a kinodynamic MPC
\citep{li_zero-shot_2022}; RMA \citep{kumar_rma_2021} adapts online from a
history of states and actions.
At the footstep level, \citet{akizhanov_learning_2024} correct an NMPC's
contact targets with a network trained on the contacts it
reached, as our inverse footstep model does, but within a fixed gait and
on one controller. \citet{taouil_quadrupedal_2023} plan footsteps with
learned motion models of a black-box trotting controller, and
\citet{jenelten_dtc_2024} reuse a learned tracker under new planners.
However, we know of no work that moves a learned gait-free footstep planner
between two model-based controllers.
