\section{Simulation Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask whether GaitNet beats OCS2's own trot
(\textbf{Q1}), what it keeps in moving to OCS2
(\textbf{Q2}), and what the refitted IFM, overlapping swings, and free
leg pairing contribute (\textbf{Q3}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:exp-setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmark.}
\looseness=-1 The benchmark runs on OCS2 in Gazebo
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}). Each world is a strip of
\corlBenchTileCm\,cm tiles, the training tile size, each a solid pillar
or, with probability equal to the void fraction, a void. The void
fractions, \corlBenchVoidLow, \corlBenchVoidMid, and \corlBenchVoidHigh\%,
are the void areas of Isaac's worlds at nominal void fractions of
\corlBenchIsaacVoidLow, \corlBenchIsaacVoidMid, and
\corlBenchIsaacVoidHigh\%, and the commanded speeds are
\corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s. Each of these six
combinations, a \emph{cell}, holds \corlBenchTrialsPerCell{} trials of
\corlBenchTrialSeconds\,s on world seeds
\corlBenchSeedFirst--\corlBenchSeedLast, so each method runs
\corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} trials on the same worlds. A trial succeeds if
the robot does not fall and walks at least half the commanded distance.
None of these world seeds were used to log the OCS2 IFM's data or to
develop the depth pipeline.

\noindent\textbf{Training seeds.}
\looseness=-1 We train every learned method from scratch
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}) with \corlNumTrainSeeds{} training seeds and
evaluate each seed at iteration \corlOcsCheckpoint, where GaitNet's mean
success over its seeds on the training controller in Isaac was highest.
This iteration was fixed before any trial on OCS2 and is used for every
learned method; Q2's Isaac numbers come from this same evaluation
(training time: App.~\ref{app:training}). Every seed runs all
trials; we report seed means. For Q2, GaitNet and three other methods run on the training controller in
Isaac, randomized as in training, at
matching nominal void fractions, \corlBenchIsaacRobots{} robots per
cell, one trial each, and the same criterion.

\noindent\textbf{Methods (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}, Table~\ref{tab:results}).}
\looseness=-1 All methods share the depth pipeline, NMPC, and WBC.
\emph{GaitNet} runs as deployed, with the OCS2 IFM and up to two legs
swinging in any pair. \emph{OCS2's own trot} is
the stock perceptive trot of \citet{leggedperceptive}, which lifts each diagonal pair on a fixed schedule,
with \corlTrotSwing\,s swings and a \corlTrotPeriod\,s period. Its
footholds come from a velocity-feedback heuristic, projected onto the same
terrain map and constrained to the planar region they fall in
\citep{grandia_perceptive_2022}. \emph{OCS2's static walk}, also stock, lifts one leg at a
time in a fixed order, with \corlWalkSwing\,s swings and a
\corlWalkPeriod\,s period, so three feet are always down. \emph{GaitNet-Diagonal} uses GaitNet's weights
but starts a second swing only on the diagonal partner of the swinging
leg. \emph{Per-candidate + IFM} is the per-candidate network of
\citet{Sullivan2025} (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}). We trained it
with GaitNet's recipe but, as in \citet{Sullivan2025}, without the IFM in the loop, and ran it with
the OCS2 IFM.
\emph{GaitNet without the IFM} sends
GaitNet's desired foothold unchanged.
\emph{GaitNet + training IFM} sends it through the training
controller's IFM instead of the refitted one, and \emph{GaitNet + offset}
adds to the desired foothold a constant offset per leg, the mean of
command minus landing over the OCS2 IFM's logged steps.

\noindent\textbf{Sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}).}
% Author, 2026-11-04: same checkpoint, same worlds for every method,
% 12.5 cm tiles, 80 trials per point; GaitNet-Single trained one-swing.
\looseness=-1 The sweep uses the same
checkpoints and \corlBenchTileCm\,cm tiles, at eight nominal void
fractions (0 to \corlOcsMaxVoidPct\%; above \corlMaxVoidPct\%, beyond the
training curriculum) and five speeds (\corlOcsSpeedMin{} to
\corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s), every method on the same worlds. Each method runs
\corlOcsTrialsPerPoint{} trials per void fraction, spread over the
speeds, and some also per speed (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}b). A trial succeeds if the robot reaches the end of a
\corlOcsTrackM\,m track within \corlOcsTimeFactor{} times the time needed
at the commanded speed. The sweep also runs Per-candidate + IFM,
GaitNet-Diagonal, and \emph{GaitNet-Single}, trained with GaitNet's
recipe but with one swing at a time.

\noindent\textbf{Q1. Against OCS2's own trot.}
\looseness=-1 On the benchmark, GaitNet succeeded in \corlBenchFull\% of
trials against the trot's \corlBenchTrot\% (Table~\ref{tab:results}), a lead of 7.5 points. OCS2's static walk, which always keeps
three feet down, does not close it: it succeeded in \corlBenchStaticWalk\% overall and
\corlBenchStaticWalkDense\% at \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids, 11.0 and 21.0
points below GaitNet (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}a,\,b). GaitNet's lead over
the trot grew with the void fraction at both speeds, from 3 and 2 points at
26\% voids to 16 and 14 points at \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids, which held
two thirds of it. Since all three share the terrain map, NMPC, and WBC,
the lead comes from GaitNet and its execution layer, which differ from
the trot in when they step, where, and how commands are corrected.

\noindent\textbf{Q2. From the training controller to OCS2.}
\looseness=-1 The frozen GaitNet succeeded in \corlBenchFull\% on OCS2
against \corlBenchIsaacIdm\% on the training controller in Isaac, although
the controller, simulator, odometry, and terrain source all changed; the
loss was largest at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s (\corlBenchFullFast{} against
94.7\%) and in the hardest cell (77 against 89.2\%). GaitNet-Single and
Per-candidate + IFM instead scored higher on OCS2 (Q3).

\noindent\phantomsection\label{subsec:exp-why}\textbf{Q3. The refitted IFM.}
\looseness=-1 Three controls keep GaitNet's weights and change how its
footholds are corrected, and with it the touchdown errors its history
reports (Table~\ref{tab:results}). Without the IFM,
GaitNet succeeded in \corlBenchNoIdm\% of trials on OCS2, far below the
trot, and in \corlBenchIsaacNoIdm\% on the training controller, so
this arm tests dependence on an IFM, and the training controller's IFM
tests transfer. With the
training controller's IFM, it succeeded in
\corlBenchTrainIfm\%, barely above no IFM, and with a
constant offset per leg in \corlBenchOffset\%. The refitted IFM, not these alternatives,
therefore carried the transfer. A flat-ground test without
voids, at \corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, shows the
refit's effect on landing: OCS2's RMS landing error was
\corlOcsLandRmsNoIdm\,cm without the IFM, three times the
\corlMarginCm\,cm candidate margin, and \corlOcsLandRmsIdm\,cm with it,
about the margin.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Leg pairs and gait.}
\looseness=-1 Free to pair any legs and given no gait reward, GaitNet
chose the trot's diagonal pairs in its successful trials but timed its
steps on its own (Fig.~\ref{fig:gait}). Of its \corlGaitOverlapPairs{}
overlapping swing pairs, \corlGaitDiagPairPct\% paired diagonal legs, and
these partners lifted off a median \corlGaitLiftLagMs\,ms apart, about
one tick. A leg's time
between lift-offs varied far more than the trot's (coefficient
of variation \corlGaitStrideCv{} against \corlGaitStrideCvTrot).
Restricted to the trot's pairing at test time, GaitNet-Diagonal
succeeded in \corlBenchDiag\% (Table~\ref{tab:results}) and lost most at \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids,
7 and 18 points at \corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}), where safe footholds were scarcest.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Overlapping swings.}
\looseness=-1 Trained to swing one leg at a time, GaitNet-Single
succeeded in \corlSweepSingle\% of the sweep's trials against GaitNet's
\corlSweepFull\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}), less
often at every void fraction and speed. It also trailed GaitNet on the
benchmark (\corlBenchSingle{} against \corlBenchFull\%) and on the
training controller (\corlBenchIsaacSingle{} against
\corlBenchIsaacIdm\%), so the gap is not due to the transfer. It
took \corlOcsStepsPerMSingle{} steps per meter against
\corlOcsStepsPerMFull{} for GaitNet in successful trials. Swinging
one leg at a time, for at least \corlSwingDurationMin\,s and starting on
ticks, it can take about eight steps per second, enough up to about
0.15\,m/s, so this cap does not explain its 21-point gap at 0.10\,m/s. At
equal training budget, overlapping swings raised success on these voids.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Per-candidate network.}
\looseness=-1 Per-candidate + IFM, the network GaitNet reduces to at
initialization (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}), succeeded in
\corlBenchMlpMean\% on the benchmark, in \corlSweepMlp\% in the sweep,
and in \corlBenchIsaacMlp\% on the training controller, 7.0, 9.1, and
15.7 points below GaitNet (Table~\ref{tab:results}). The two differ
in the mixer, the history, size (App.~\ref{app:settings}), and the IFM
in training, so these gaps measure all four together.
