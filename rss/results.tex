\section{Simulation Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask whether GaitNet beats OCS2's own trot
(\textbf{Q1}), what it loses in moving to OCS2
(\textbf{Q2}), and how much it needs the IDM, overlapping swings, non-diagonal pairs,
history, and mixer (\textbf{Q3}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:exp-setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmark.}
\looseness=-1 The benchmark runs on OCS2 in Gazebo
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}). Each world is a strip of
\corlBenchTileCm\,cm tiles, the training tile size, each a solid pillar
or, with probability equal to the void fraction, a void. The void
fractions, \corlBenchVoidLow, \corlBenchVoidMid, and \corlBenchVoidHigh\%,
are the void areas of Isaac's worlds at nominal void fractions of
\corlBenchIsaacVoidLow, \corlBenchIsaacVoidMid, and
\corlBenchIsaacVoidHigh\%, and the commanded speeds are
\corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s. Each of these six
combinations, a \emph{cell}, holds \corlBenchTrialsPerCell{} trials of
\corlBenchTrialSeconds\,s on world seeds
\corlBenchSeedFirst--\corlBenchSeedLast, so each run of a method covers
\corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} trials, on the same worlds as every other
method. A trial succeeds if
the robot does not fall and walks at least half the commanded distance.
None of these world seeds was used to log the OCS2 IDM's data or to
develop the depth pipeline.

\noindent\textbf{Training seeds.}
\looseness=-1 We train every learned method from scratch
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}) with \corlNumTrainSeeds{} training seeds and
evaluate each seed at iteration \corlOcsCheckpoint, fixed before any trial
on OCS2 (training time: App.~\ref{app:training}). Every seed runs all
trials, and we report the mean over the \corlNumTrainSeeds{} seeds. For Q2, GaitNet also runs on the training controller in Isaac at
matching nominal void fractions, with \corlBenchIsaacRobots{} robots per
cell, one trial each, and the same criterion.

\noindent\textbf{Methods (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}, Table~\ref{tab:results}).}
\looseness=-1 All methods share the depth pipeline, NMPC, and WBC.
\emph{GaitNet} runs as deployed, with the OCS2 IDM and up to two legs
swinging in any pair. \emph{OCS2's own trot} is
OCS2's perceptive trot, which lifts each diagonal pair (a front leg and the opposite rear leg) on a fixed schedule,
with its own footholds, \corlTrotSwing\,s swings, and a
\corlTrotPeriod\,s period. \emph{GaitNet-Diagonal} uses GaitNet's weights
but starts a second swing only on the diagonal partner of the swinging
leg. \emph{Per-candidate + IDM} is the per-candidate network of
\citet{Sullivan2025} (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}). We trained it
with GaitNet's recipe but without the IDM in the loop, and ran it with
the OCS2 IDM.
\emph{GaitNet without the IDM} uses
GaitNet's weights and sends its desired foothold unchanged.

\noindent\textbf{Retrained ablations (Table~\ref{tab:retrain}).}
\looseness=-1 We retrained GaitNet under a separate training recipe,
with \corlNumTrainSeeds{} training seeds per version, and ran each on the benchmark. \emph{Full retrain} keeps every part, while \emph{No
history}, \emph{No candidate mixer}, and \emph{No IDM in training} each
drop one part. Since their recipe differs from GaitNet's, the
full retrain is the reference for the others. We report means only,
so differences of a few points may not hold across training seeds. No IDM in
training runs on OCS2 twice, with no IDM and with the training
controller's (Isaac) IDM, and the others with \verify{the OCS2 IDM}.

\noindent\textbf{Sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}).}
% Author, 2026-11-04: same checkpoint, same worlds for every method,
% 12.5 cm tiles, 80 trials per point; GaitNet-Single trained one-swing.
\looseness=-1 The sweep is a second set of trials with the same
checkpoints and \corlBenchTileCm\,cm tiles, at eight nominal void
fractions (0 to \corlOcsMaxVoidPct\%; above \corlMaxVoidPct\%, beyond the
training curriculum) and five speeds (\corlOcsSpeedMin{} to
\corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s), every method on the same worlds. Each method runs
\corlOcsTrialsPerPoint{} trials per void fraction, spread over the
speeds, and some also per speed (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}b). A trial succeeds if the robot reaches the end of a
\corlOcsTrackM\,m track within \corlOcsTimeFactor{} times the time needed
at the commanded speed. The sweep also runs GaitNet without the IDM, Per-candidate + IDM,
GaitNet-Diagonal, and \emph{GaitNet-Single}, trained with GaitNet's
recipe but with one swing at a time (also run on the benchmark).

\noindent\textbf{Q1. Against OCS2's own trot.}
\looseness=-1 On the benchmark, GaitNet succeeded in \corlBenchFull\% of
trials against the trot's \corlBenchTrot\% (Table~\ref{tab:results},
Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}c), a gap of 7.5 percentage points.
Since both methods share the terrain, NMPC, and WBC, the gap comes from
the planner and execution layer together, not from free leg pairing alone
(Q3). The gap was small at \corlBenchVoidLow{} and
\corlBenchVoidMid\% voids and largest at \corlBenchVoidHigh\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}a,\,b, App.~\ref{app:counts}), which held two
thirds of the gap. We
conjecture that the trot's fixed schedule can force a step with no safe
foothold in reach, while GaitNet can wait, move another leg, or step
farther.

\noindent\textbf{Q2. From the training controller to OCS2.}
\looseness=-1 The frozen GaitNet kept most of its success in moving to
OCS2, with \corlBenchFull\% against \corlBenchIsaacIdm\% on the training
controller in Isaac (Table~\ref{tab:results}). Most of the loss came at
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s (\corlBenchFullFast{} against 94.7\%), and the
largest, 12 points, in the hardest cell (App.~\ref{app:counts}). The gap combines all changes of Problem~2, which we do not
separate. As iteration \corlOcsCheckpoint{} was \verify{chosen on the training
controller in Isaac}, its Isaac success may be optimistic.

\noindent\phantomsection\label{subsec:exp-why}\textbf{Q3. IDM.}
\looseness=-1 Without the IDM, the frozen GaitNet's success fell to
\corlBenchNoIdm\% on OCS2, far below the trot, and similarly on the
training controller (Table~\ref{tab:results}). It did better in the sweep at the
same nominal void fractions (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}a), a difference we have not explained. In a
separate flat-ground test without voids at \corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s,
OCS2's landing error, as the RMS of its length, was
\corlOcsLandRmsNoIdm\,cm without the IDM, several times the
\corlMarginCm\,cm candidate margin, and \corlOcsLandRmsIdm\,cm with it,
equal to the margin. Removing the IDM
also changes GaitNet's inputs, since its history reports touchdown errors
relative to the IDM's output. This ablation therefore measures GaitNet's
dependence on the IDM, not the IDM's value alone.

% and 337/600 = 56.2 (both 768/1200 = 64.0 over all cells); full retrain
% 92 and 72.5 (per-command values only). Every seed of both no-IDM arms is

\looseness=-1 Retraining without the IDM asks a different question:
whether a policy trained without it does as well, not how much the trained
policy depends on it. Retrained and run without the IDM
(Table~\ref{tab:retrain}), GaitNet trailed the full retrain
at both speeds. Adding the training controller's IDM at deployment did
not help overall: success fell at \corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s and rose by
as much at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s. As we did not run this version with
the OCS2 IDM, the comparison with the full retrain shows that training and
deploying with the IDM helped on OCS2, not that it must be in the
training loop.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Leg pairs and gait.}
\looseness=-1 In its successful trials (App.~\ref{app:gait}), GaitNet, free to pair
any legs, kept the trot's pairing but timed its steps on its own
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gait}). Of its overlapping swings,
\corlGaitDiagPairPct\% paired a front leg with the opposite rear leg, and
these partners lifted off a median \corlGaitLiftLagMs\,ms apart, about
one tick (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-step}). The time
between a leg's lift-offs varied far more than the trot's (coefficient
of variation \corlGaitStrideCv{} against \corlGaitStrideCvTrot).
GaitNet-Diagonal trailed GaitNet by 4.3 points
(Table~\ref{tab:results}), mostly in the hardest cell
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}b), and stayed about three points above the trot,
too close to rank them. Never trained
with this restriction, GaitNet may rely on non-diagonal pairs on dense
voids at the faster speed; free pairing need not be better in general.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Overlapping swings.}
\looseness=-1 Trained to swing one leg at a time, GaitNet-Single
succeeded in \corlSweepSingle\% of the sweep's trials against GaitNet's
\corlSweepFull\% (Table~\ref{tab:results}, Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}), less
often at every void fraction and speed, and also trailed it on the
benchmark. It took shorter steps,
\corlOcsStepsPerMSingle{} per metre against \corlOcsStepsPerMFull{} for
GaitNet in successful trials. Its one swing at a time, of at least
\corlSwingDurationMin\,s and started on ticks, caps it near eight steps
per second, too few only from about 0.20\,m/s, so the cap cannot explain
its 21-point gap at 0.10\,m/s. Averaged over
\corlNumTrainSeeds{} training seeds each, this suggests that overlapping swings helped on these voids.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. History and candidate mixer.}
\looseness=-1 Retrained without the history
(Table~\ref{tab:retrain}), GaitNet matched the full retrain at
\corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s but fell well behind at
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s. The retrained GaitNet thus needed the
history only at the faster speed, for unmeasured reasons. Without the
mixer, it trailed the full retrain at \corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s and matched it
at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, so the mixer's effect is open.

\noindent\textbf{Per-candidate network.}
\looseness=-1 Per-candidate + IDM succeeded about as often as the trot on
the benchmark and trailed GaitNet by 7 points (Table~\ref{tab:results}).
The two networks also differ in size (App.~\ref{app:settings}) and in
the IDM in training, so this gap is not the effect of the mixer and
history alone.
