\looseness=-1 We test whether CADENCE beats OCS2's own cyclic gaits on
OCS2, what gait emerged and which freedoms of its action space
mattered, and what moving it from the convex MPC to OCS2 took
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-transfer}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:exp-setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmark.}
\looseness=-1 The benchmark runs on OCS2 in Gazebo
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}) on strips of
\corlBenchTileCm\,cm tiles, each a solid pillar or, with probability
equal to the void fraction, a void. Its six \emph{cells} combine void
fractions of \corlBenchVoidLow, \corlBenchVoidMid, and
\corlBenchVoidHigh\% (the void areas of Isaac's nominal
\corlBenchIsaacVoidLow, \corlBenchIsaacVoidMid, and
\corlBenchIsaacVoidHigh\%) with speeds of \corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, and each cell holds
\corlBenchTrialsPerCell{} trials of \corlBenchTrialSeconds\,s on the
same worlds for every method. A trial succeeds if the robot does not
fall and walks at least half the nominal distance, the speed times the
trial time. Every learned method is trained with
\corlNumTrainSeeds{} training seeds and evaluated at iteration
\corlOcsCheckpoint, where CADENCE's mean success on the training
controller in Isaac was highest; this iteration was fixed before any
result trials and may not be the best one for the
other methods. We report
the mean, lowest, and
highest seed scores. A sweep adds more void fractions and speeds on other
worlds, with a stricter criterion (App.~\ref{app:sweep}).

\noindent\textbf{Methods.}
\looseness=-1 Here we detail nine methods shown in Table~\ref{tab:results}: CADENCE, 
two cyclic gaits from OCS2, and six ablations of CADENCE. 
All methods share the depth map, NMPC, and WBC.
OCS2-Trot and OCS2-Single are the stock cyclic gaits
of \citet{leggedperceptive}. The default OCS2 gait parameters were used, having a \corlTrotSwing\,s swing time, a \corlTrotPeriod\,s period for the trot, and
a \corlWalkPeriod\,s period for the static gait (OCS2-Single). OCS2 chooses footholds from planar regions
using a heuristic \citep{grandia_perceptive_2022}.
% OCS2's gaits were not tuned, using the default 
% settings where the trot lifts diagonal
% pairs with \corlTrotSwing\,s swings on a \corlTrotPeriod\,s period, the
% walk one leg at a time with \corlWalkSwing\,s swings on a
% \corlWalkPeriod\,s period, both with heuristic footholds projected onto
% planar regions \citep{grandia_perceptive_2022}. 
% They ran once each.
CADENCE runs with its refitted OCS2 IFM and lets any two legs
swing at once. CADENCE-without-mixer-or-history scores each candidate
alone from the state and its code (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network})
and is trained with CADENCE's recipe but with no IFM in the training
loop. CADENCE-Diagonal keeps CADENCE's weights but, at
test time, starts a second swing only on the swinging leg's diagonal
partner, and CADENCE-Single is retrained to swing one leg at a
time. Both CADENCE-without-mixer-or-history and CADENCE-Single run with the OCS2 IFM
of the CADENCE policy with the same training seed, which was fitted on
that policy's steps and not on their own.
Three ablations keep CADENCE's weights and change only the footstep
correction. CADENCE-with-offset adds a constant per-leg offset, the
mean of command minus landing over the OCS2 IFM's logged steps.
CADENCE-without-IFM sends the desired foothold unchanged,
and CADENCE-with-training-IFM uses the training controller's
IFM.

\noindent\textbf{Gait data.}
\looseness=-1 The gait statistics come from the successful trials of an
earlier evaluation run of one CADENCE policy
(\corlGaitTrialsGaitNet{} of \corlBenchTrialsPerArm) and of the trot
(\corlGaitTrialsTrot) on other worlds, not from the runs of
Table~\ref{tab:results} (App.~\ref{app:earlier-run}). A fixed period
gives a near-zero coefficient of variation (CV) of a leg's time between
lift-offs, and a fixed order that lifts each leg once per cycle, at
most two at a time, never repeats a \emph{step event} (the legs lifting
within \corlGaitEventWindowMs\,ms of its first lift-off) immediately,
nor lifts a leg twice in a row.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.}
\looseness=-1 We report success rates and their differences in
percentage points, and, on flat ground at \corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, OCS2's RMS landing error, the distance from
the desired foothold to the landing.

\subsection{Results}
\label{subsec:exp-transfer}



\noindent\textbf{CADENCE beat OCS2's cyclic gaits, most where
voids are densest (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}, Table~\ref{tab:results}).}
\looseness=-1 CADENCE succeeded in \corlBenchFull\% of benchmark
trials, against \corlBenchTrot\% for OCS2-Trot and
\corlBenchStaticWalk\% for OCS2-Single, leads of 17.3 and 19.6
percentage points, and even its lowest seed led the trot by 10.5. The
lead over the trot grew with the void fraction, from 2 to 18 to 30
points at \corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s and from 4 to 20 to 30 at
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, although the walk beat CADENCE in the easiest
cell (94 against 93\%). In the hardest cell, \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids
at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, CADENCE succeeded in 70\% against 40\% for
the trot and 35\% for the walk (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}b). CADENCE-without-mixer-or-history succeeded in
\corlBenchMlpMean\%, 13.8 points below CADENCE, from which it differs in
the mixer, the history, network size, the IFM in training, and an OCS2
IFM fitted on CADENCE's steps. Since the stock gaits
also take footholds from another source, the lead belongs to CADENCE and
its execution layer together.

\looseness=-1 The sweep, on other worlds and with a criterion that also
fails a robot that stalls, extends this result beyond training. In
Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep-main}a, CADENCE reached \corlSweepFull\% against
the trot's \corlSweepTrot\%. The two were
equal up to a nominal 10\%, and from 30\% on CADENCE led by 15 to 42
points. Beyond the \corlMaxVoidPct\% curriculum, at 50, 60, and
\corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids, it succeeded in 80, 60, and 50\% against
the trot's 38, 30, and 12\%. It also led at every speed, and at
\corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s, above the training speeds, it succeeded in 66
against 51\%. CADENCE therefore kept its lead on unseen worlds, denser
voids, and a higher speed than it was trained on.

\noindent\textbf{The emergent gait is acyclic often pairs diagonal legs
(Figs.~\ref{fig:gait} and~\ref{fig:swing-transfer}a).}
\looseness=-1 With no gait schedule, gait reward, or phase variable,
CADENCE's gait had neither a fixed period nor a fixed order. In the
example trial of Fig.~\ref{fig:gait}a, the trot alternated its two
diagonal pairs on its fixed period, whereas CADENCE spaced its steps
unevenly and sometimes split a pair into single-leg steps. Over all
successful trials, a leg's time between lift-offs varied far more than
in the trot (CV \corlGaitStrideCv{} against \corlGaitStrideCvTrot;
Fig.~\ref{fig:gait}b), and CADENCE raised its step rate with speed, from
\corlGaitStepsSlow{} steps per second at \corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s to
\corlGaitStepsFast{} at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, while the trot kept
\corlGaitStepsTrot{} at both. A step event repeated the legs of the
one before it in \corlGaitEventRepeatPct\% of consecutive events, and a
leg lifted twice in a row in \corlGaitLegTwicePct\% of consecutive
lift-offs, against \corlGaitEventRepeatPctTrot{} and
\corlGaitLegTwicePctTrot\% for the trot, and four consecutive lift-offs
covered all four legs in \corlGaitFourAllPct{} against
\corlGaitFourAllPctTrot\%. CADENCE's measured swings were shorter
(median \corlGaitSwingMedian{} against \corlGaitSwingMedianTrot\,s;
Fig.~\ref{fig:swing-transfer}a). Yet \corlGaitDiagPairPct\% of its
\corlGaitOverlapPairs{} overlapping swing pairs were diagonal
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gait}c), with partners lifting off a median
\corlGaitLiftLagMs\,ms apart, about one tick. CADENCE therefore kept
the trot's pairing but neither its period nor its order, in this one
run of one policy.

\looseness=-1 Restricting the action space tested which freedom
mattered. Retrained to swing one leg at a time, CADENCE-Single
succeeded in only \corlBenchSingle\%, though OCS2-Single, also
one leg at a time, reached \corlBenchStaticWalk\%. This loss mixes the
missing overlap with an OCS2 IFM fitted on CADENCE's two-leg steps. Restricted at test time to diagonal
pairs, CADENCE-Diagonal lost only 2.2 points on the benchmark (1.2 to
2.8 in each seed), but 9.4 in the sweep (\corlSweepDiag\%, not shown).

\noindent\phantomsection\label{subsec:exp-why}\textbf{Moving CADENCE to
OCS2 took a refitted footstep model (Fig.~\ref{fig:swing-transfer}b).}
\looseness=-1 With CADENCE's weights frozen, it succeeded in
\corlBenchNoIdm\% without the IFM and in only \corlBenchTrainIfm\% with
the training controller's IFM, consistent with that IFM cancelling a
hip-fixed overshoot that OCS2's world-fixed target lacks
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}). A constant per-leg offset recovered much
of the loss (\corlBenchOffset\%), and the refitted IFM added 25.1
points more; we did not test models between the two. Overall, only the
refit lifted CADENCE above the trot, although at \corlBenchVoidHigh\%
voids the offset also beat it (54 and 47 against 46 and 40\%).
\verify{On flat ground, the refit cut OCS2's RMS landing error from
about \corlOcsLandRmsNoIdm\,cm, three times the \corlMarginCm\,cm
margin, to about \corlOcsLandRmsIdm\,cm (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}c).}
