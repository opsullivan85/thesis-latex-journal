\section{Simulation Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask: (Q1)~does the frozen GaitNet outperform OCS2's own trot as voids
get denser and commands faster; (Q2)~does it benefit from swinging more
than one leg at a time; and (Q3)~how much do the IDM and the comparison
between candidates contribute?

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:exp-setup}

We run GaitNet on OCS2 in Gazebo (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}),
with the IDM fitted on OCS2's own steps (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}).
All GaitNet variants use one checkpoint, from about iteration
\verify{\corlOcsCheckpoint} of a seeded run trained from scratch with the
IDM in the loop (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}), frozen after
training, with its seed and iteration fixed before any trial on OCS2. Each world is a strip of
pillars in which each tile is a real drop with probability equal to
the void fraction. We run two sweeps as separate sets of trials: one over eight
void fractions, from 0 to \corlOcsMaxVoidPct\%, and one over five
forward speeds, from \corlOcsSpeedMin{} to \corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s, with
\corlOcsTrialsPerPoint{} trials per point (all five methods in the void
sweep; GaitNet, GaitNet-Single and OCS2's own trot in the speed sweep).
Each point
of the void sweep spreads its trials over all five speeds
\verify{(the void fractions of the speed sweep to be stated)}. All
methods run on the same worlds. The
world seeds are new, disjoint from those that tuned the execution
layer, supplied the IDM's data, or were used to develop the camera
pipeline. The fastest command lies above the \corlCmdVxMax\,m/s that
GaitNet was trained on. The tiles are larger than the training
cells (\corlGzTileCm{} against \corlTerrainCellCm\,cm), so void
fractions in training and on OCS2 are not directly comparable. A trial succeeds if the robot reaches the end
of a \corlOcsTrackM\,m track without falling or leaving the strip,
within \corlOcsTimeFactor{} times the time the command needs, from
90\,s at \corlOcsSpeedMin\,m/s to 18\,s at \corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s.

We compare five methods, all on the same worlds, depth pipeline, NMPC
and WBC, differing only in how steps are chosen and executed:
\emph{GaitNet} as deployed, with up to two legs swinging at once;
\emph{OCS2's own trot}, its perceptive trot with its own gait scheduler
and footholds; \emph{GaitNet-Single}, the same weights with only one leg
swinging at a time; \emph{GaitNet, no IDM}, the same weights with the
chosen landing sent to OCS2 unchanged; and \emph{Per-candidate + IDM},
the per-candidate network of \citet{Sullivan2025}
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}), which scores each candidate on its
own and has no history, trained with GaitNet's recipe and the IDM in the
loop and run with the same OCS2 IDM (the first of several seeded runs,
chosen before evaluation).
With \corlOcsTrialsPerPoint{} trials per point, a success rate between
30 and 70\% carries a 95\% Wilson interval of about $\pm$10 points. We
compare methods with two-sided Fisher exact tests at single points and,
for pooled comparisons, with Cochran--Mantel--Haenszel tests stratified by
void fraction or speed. These tests treat the methods' trials as
independent although all methods run on the same worlds; a paired test on
per-trial outcomes would usually be more sensitive. Single-point
$p$-values are not corrected for multiple comparisons; the pooled tests
are the primary ones, and all tests compare fixed checkpoints, so they
leave out variation across training seeds.

\subsection{Comparison with OCS2's Own Trot}
\label{subsec:exp-transfer}

GaitNet matches OCS2's own trot on sparse voids and gains a lead as the
voids get denser (Fig.~\ref{fig:ocs2-results}a). Both succeed in every
trial up to 10\% voids and are two trials apart at 20\%. From 30\%
voids on, GaitNet leads at every void fraction ($p=\corlOcsPTrotThirty$
at 30\%), with \corlOcsDenseFull\% [\corlOcsDenseFullCiLow,
\corlOcsDenseFullCiHigh] against \corlOcsDenseTrot\%
[\corlOcsDenseTrotCiLow, \corlOcsDenseTrotCiHigh] success over
40--\corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids ($p<\corlOcsPTrotDense$). The gap is
largest from 50\% voids on, where GaitNet still succeeds in 50--80\% of the trials, and at
\corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids in half of them, against 12--38\% for OCS2's
own trot.

Because both see the same terrain and run on the same NMPC and WBC,
this gap comes from how steps are chosen and executed: without the IDM,
GaitNet does no better than the trot over 40--\corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids
(\corlOcsDenseNoIdm\% against \corlOcsDenseTrot\%;
Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-why}). Our interpretation, which these trials do
not test, is that on dense voids the trot, lifting each diagonal pair on
a fixed schedule, is often forced to step when no good foothold is in
reach, whereas GaitNet can wait, move another leg first, or take a
longer step.

GaitNet also leads at every commanded speed
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ocs2-results}b), by 5 to 17.5 points
($p<\corlOcsPTrotSpeed$ pooled over speeds), although at a single speed
only the 17.5-point gap at 0.20\,m/s is significant on its own
($p=\corlOcsPTrotSpeedTwenty$, uncorrected). Both lose success at the
higher commands, most at \corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s,
above the range GaitNet was trained on, yet GaitNet still succeeds in
two thirds of those trials.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{subsec:exp-why}

The frozen GaitNet depends on overlapping swings (Q2). Under a one-swing limit, GaitNet still
walks, but it succeeds less often at every void fraction and every
speed, \corlOcsAllSingle\% against \corlOcsAllFull\% on average over
void fractions ($p<\corlOcsPSingle$); the gap is 10 points at
\corlOcsSpeedMin\,m/s and more than 20 from 0.10\,m/s on. It also takes
shorter steps:
\corlOcsStepsPerMSingle{} steps per metre against
\corlOcsStepsPerMFull{} for GaitNet. With one leg in the air at a time,
swings of at least \corlSwingDurationMin\,s, and steps that start only
on \corlControlTickMs\,ms ticks, it cannot start more than about eight
steps per second, yet at \corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s, with steps this short,
it would need about eleven. This cap binds only from about 0.20\,m/s,
so it can explain why GaitNet-Single falls behind the command there, but
not its 21-point lower success already at 0.10\,m/s. Because GaitNet was never trained under this
limit, these results show that GaitNet relies on swinging
two legs together, not that overlap is better in general.

The frozen GaitNet depends on the IDM (Q3). Without it, GaitNet's success falls from
\corlOcsAllFull\% to \corlOcsAllNoIdm\% on average over void
fractions ($p<\corlOcsPNoIdm$), below GaitNet-Single at 20--50\% voids. Only on ground
without voids, where a missed landing costs little, does GaitNet do
well without it; already at 10\% voids it loses 20 points. GaitNet
chooses cells that are safe with a margin of \corlMarginCm\,cm in
training and slightly more on OCS2 (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}).
Without the IDM, OCS2 lands the foot \corlOcsLandRmsNoIdm\,cm RMS from the
chosen cell, several times the training margin, so a foot aimed next to
a void can end up in it; with the IDM, the error drops to
\corlOcsLandRmsIdm\,cm, about the training margin, in a separate test on
flat ground \verify{(speeds to be stated)}. Removing the IDM also changes GaitNet's inputs,
since its history reports touchdown errors measured against the IDM's
output, so this ablation measures how much the frozen GaitNet depends
on the IDM, not the value of the IDM alone.

The per-candidate network falls behind on dense voids. It
was trained with the same recipe and runs with the same IDM, but
scores each candidate on its own and has no motion history. It stays
within 8 points of GaitNet up to 20\% voids but falls behind from 30\%
on, with \corlOcsDenseMlp\% against \corlOcsDenseFull\% over
40--\corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids ($p=\corlOcsPMlpDense$), yet it still beats
OCS2's own trot there (\corlOcsDenseMlp\% against \corlOcsDenseTrot\%),
so much of GaitNet's lead over the trot is already reached by the
earlier network trained with the same recipe and IDM. The gap is
largest, and significant on its own, at 30 and 40\% voids
($p=\corlOcsPMlpThirty$ and $\corlOcsPMlpForty$); at the densest
voids, where both often fail, it shrinks and no single void fraction
shows a significant difference. The
comparison changes the candidate mixer, the history and the network
size (\corlParamsPerCandidateK{}k against \corlParamsGaitNetK{}k
parameters) at once, and each network is a single run, so it does not
show which of these matters.
