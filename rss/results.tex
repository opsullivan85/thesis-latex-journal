\section{Simulation Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask whether GaitNet beats OCS2's own trot
(\textbf{Q1}), what it loses in moving to OCS2
(\textbf{Q2}), and how much it needs the IDM, overlapping swings, non-diagonal pairs,
history, and mixer (\textbf{Q3}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:exp-setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmark.}
\looseness=-1 The benchmark runs on OCS2 in Gazebo
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}). Each world is a strip of
\corlBenchTileCm\,cm tiles, the training tile size, each a solid pillar
or, with probability equal to the void fraction, a void. The void
fractions, \corlBenchVoidLow, \corlBenchVoidMid, and \corlBenchVoidHigh\%,
are the void areas of Isaac's worlds at nominal void fractions of
\corlBenchIsaacVoidLow, \corlBenchIsaacVoidMid, and
\corlBenchIsaacVoidHigh\%, and the commanded speeds are
\corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s. Each of these six
combinations, a \emph{cell}, holds \corlBenchTrialsPerCell{} trials of
\corlBenchTrialSeconds\,s on world seeds
\corlBenchSeedFirst--\corlBenchSeedLast, so each run of a method covers
\corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} trials, on the same worlds as every other
method. A trial succeeds if
the robot does not fall and walks at least half the commanded distance.
None of these world seeds was used to tune the lead and candidate margin,
log the OCS2 IDM's data, or develop the depth pipeline \verify{(confirm
for the 12.5\,cm worlds)}.

\noindent\textbf{GaitNet's checkpoint.}
\looseness=-1 We evaluate iteration \corlOcsCheckpoint{} of one of
\corlNumSeedsGaitNet{} runs trained from scratch
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}) with different training seeds. The
run was picked at random, and the checkpoint was fixed \verify{before any
trial on OCS2}. It took about \corlTrainHours\,h to train on one
\verify{NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell} GPU. For Q2, it also runs on the training controller in Isaac at
matching nominal void fractions, with \corlBenchIsaacRobots{} robots per
cell, one trial each, and the same criterion.

\noindent\textbf{Methods (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}).}
\looseness=-1 All methods share the depth pipeline, NMPC, and WBC.
\emph{GaitNet} runs as deployed, with the OCS2 IDM and up to two legs
swinging in any pair. \emph{GaitNet rerun} is the checkpoint run
again, to measure run-to-run variation. \emph{OCS2's own trot} is
OCS2's perceptive trot, which lifts each diagonal pair (a front leg and the opposite rear leg) on a fixed schedule,
with its own footholds, \verify{\corlTrotSwing\,s swings}, and a
\corlTrotPeriod\,s period. \emph{GaitNet-Diagonal} uses GaitNet's weights
but starts a second swing only on the diagonal partner of the swinging
leg. \emph{Per-candidate + IDM} is the per-candidate network of
\citet{Sullivan2025} (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}). We trained
four networks, one per training seed, \verify{with GaitNet's
recipe and the IDM in the loop} for about \corlBenchMlpIterations{}
iterations, more than GaitNet's \corlOcsCheckpoint, and ran each with
\verify{the OCS2 IDM}. \emph{GaitNet without the IDM}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}a) sends its desired foothold
unchanged.

\noindent\textbf{Retrained ablations (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}b).}
\looseness=-1 We retrained GaitNet under a separate training recipe,
with two training seeds per version, and ran each on the benchmark. \emph{Full retrain} keeps every part, while \emph{No
history}, \emph{No candidate mixer}, and \emph{No IDM in training} each
drop one part. Since their recipe differs from GaitNet's, the
full retrain is the reference for the others. No IDM in
training runs on OCS2 twice, with no IDM and with the training
controller's (Isaac) IDM, and the others with \verify{the OCS2 IDM}.

\noindent\textbf{Sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}).}
\looseness=-1 The sweep, a separate protocol, runs GaitNet at eight void
fractions (0 to \corlOcsMaxVoidPct\%) and six speeds (\corlOcsSpeedMin{}
to \corlSweepSpeedMax\,m/s), with \corlSweepTrialsPerCell{} trials per
void fraction and speed \verify{on the \corlGzTileCm\,cm tiles of our
earlier benchmark}. A trial succeeds if the robot reaches the end of a
\corlOcsTrackM\,m track within \corlOcsTimeFactor{} times the time needed
at the commanded speed \verify{(criterion of the new sweep to be
confirmed)}. OCS2's own trot was run in that earlier benchmark, a previous
set of our OCS2 trials, at speeds up to \corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s with
\corlOcsTrialsPerPoint{} trials per point, on other worlds. On the
trot's worlds, it also ran an earlier GaitNet checkpoint
\verify{(about \corlEarlyCheckpoint{} training iterations)}, also without
the IDM and as \emph{GaitNet-Single}, which lets only one leg swing at a
time, and Per-candidate + IDM, one training seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}c).

\noindent\textbf{Variation.}
\looseness=-1 The rerun succeeded in \corlBenchRerun\% against GaitNet's
\corlBenchFull\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}a), so we read gaps of about two
percentage points as run-to-run variation. Training seeds of a
retrained version often differed far more, so
Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}b shows their mean, and we conclude only
from differences both share.

\subsection{Results}
\label{subsec:exp-transfer}

\noindent\textbf{Q1. Against OCS2's own trot.}
\looseness=-1 On the benchmark (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}a), GaitNet succeeded
in \corlBenchFull\% of trials against the trot's \corlBenchTrot\%. This
gap of 7.5 points is several times the run-to-run variation, and the rerun
beat the trot by about six points. Since both methods share the terrain, NMPC,
and WBC, the gap comes from how steps are chosen and executed. Per cell
(App.~\ref{app:counts}), GaitNet beat the trot by only 2 to 6 points at
\corlBenchVoidLow{} and \corlBenchVoidMid\% voids but by 16 and 14
points at \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids. There, over both speeds, it
succeeded in \corlBenchFullDense\% against \corlBenchTrotDense\%, so two
thirds of the gap came from \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids. Our
untested interpretation is that on dense voids the trot's fixed schedule
may force a step with no safe foothold in reach, whereas GaitNet can wait,
move another leg first, or take a longer step.

\looseness=-1 The sweep showed a similar trend over a wider range

earlier runs at every void fraction, with \corlOcsDenseFull\% against

\corlEarlyFull\% against the trot's \corlEarlyTrot\%.

\noindent\textbf{Q2. From the training controller to OCS2.}
\looseness=-1 The frozen GaitNet kept most of its success in moving to
OCS2 (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}a), with \corlBenchFull\% against
\corlBenchIsaacIdm\% on the training controller in Isaac. Most of the gap
came at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s (\corlBenchFullFast{} against 94.7\%).
Per cell (App.~\ref{app:counts}), it lost at most 8 points except in the
hardest cell, \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s,
where it lost 12. The gap combines all changes of Problem~2, which we do not
separate.

\noindent\phantomsection\label{subsec:exp-why}\textbf{Q3. IDM.}
\looseness=-1 Without the IDM (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}a), the
frozen GaitNet's success fell to \corlBenchNoIdm\% on OCS2, far below the
trot, and to \corlBenchIsaacNoIdm\% on the training controller. In a
separate flat-ground test without voids \verify{(speeds to be stated)},
OCS2's root-mean-square landing error was \corlOcsLandRmsNoIdm\,cm without
the IDM, several times the \corlMarginCm\,cm training candidate margin,
and \corlOcsLandRmsIdm\,cm with it, about that margin. Removing the IDM
also changes GaitNet's inputs, since its history reports touchdown errors
relative to the IDM's output. This ablation therefore measures GaitNet's
dependence on the IDM, not the IDM's value alone.

% and 337/600 = 56.2 (both 768/1200 = 64.0 over all cells); full retrain
% 92 and 72.5 (per-command values only). Every seed of both no-IDM arms is
% below both full retrains at both commands.
\looseness=-1 Retrained and run without the IDM
(Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}b), GaitNet trailed the full retrain
at both speeds, with 76.8 and 51.2\% against 92 and 72.5\%, and both
its training seeds stayed below both of the full retrain's. Adding the training controller's IDM at deployment did
not help overall, as success fell five points at \corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s
and rose five at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s. As we fit no OCS2 IDM for this
version, the comparison with the full retrain shows that training and
deploying with the IDM helped on OCS2, not that it must be in the
training loop.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Leg pairs and gait.}
\looseness=-1 In its successful trials (App.~\ref{app:gait}), GaitNet paired
legs like the trot but timed its steps on its own (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}b,\,c). Of its overlapping swings,
\corlGaitDiagPairPct\% paired a front leg with the opposite rear leg, and
these partners lifted off a median \corlGaitLiftLagMs\,ms apart, about
one tick, since GaitNet starts at most one step per tick. The time
between a leg's lift-offs varied far more than the trot's (coefficient
of variation \corlGaitStrideCv{} against \corlGaitStrideCvTrot). GaitNet
took \corlGaitStepsSlow{} and \corlGaitStepsFast{} steps per second at
\corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, against the trot's
\corlGaitStepsTrot{} at both. GaitNet-Diagonal succeeded in \corlBenchDiag\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}a), 4.3 points below GaitNet and less than three
below the rerun, a gap at the edge of run-to-run variation. Most of the
gap to GaitNet came from the hardest cell, with 59\% against 77\% for GaitNet and 74\% for its rerun
(App.~\ref{app:counts}), where GaitNet also paired other legs most often,
\corlGaitNonDiagHard{} times per successful trial against
\corlGaitNonDiagEasy{} at \corlBenchVoidLow\% voids and
\corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s, but also in \corlGaitNonDiagMildTrials{} of its
\corlGaitNonDiagMildOf{} successful trials at \corlBenchVoidLow\% voids and
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s, where the restriction cost nothing
(App.~\ref{app:gait}). Over all cells, it stayed about
three points above the trot, also near that variation. As GaitNet was
never trained with this restriction, the drop suggests that it relies on
non-diagonal pairs on dense voids at the faster speed, not that free
pairing is better in general.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Overlapping swings.}
\looseness=-1 In the earlier benchmark (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}c),
GaitNet-Single succeeded in \corlEarlySingle\% against the earlier
checkpoint's \corlEarlyFull\%, less often at every void fraction and
speed (App.~\ref{app:counts}). It took shorter steps,
\corlOcsStepsPerMSingle{} per metre against \corlOcsStepsPerMFull{} in
successful trials. One swing at a time, swings of at least
\corlSwingDurationMin\,s, and starts on ticks cap it at about eight steps
per second, too few for such short steps only from about 0.20\,m/s, so
it cannot explain its 21-point gap at 0.10\,m/s. As GaitNet was never trained under this limit, the drop
suggests that it relies on overlapping swings, not that overlap is
better in general.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. History and candidate mixer.}
\looseness=-1 Retrained without the history
(Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}b), GaitNet matched the full retrain at
\corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s (91.2 against 92\%) but fell well behind at
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s (52.8 against 72.5\%), where both its training
seeds stayed below both of the full retrain's. GaitNet thus needed the history
only at the faster speed, for reasons we did not measure. Without the
mixer, the two training seeds disagreed, one matching the full
retrain's and the other below both, so two training seeds cannot show whether
the mixer matters.

% GaitNet minus the best per-candidate seed: 91.5 - 89.7 = 1.8 points.
% Seed spread 89.7 - 75.5 = 14.2 points; GaitNet minus the seed mean 7.0.
\noindent\textbf{Per-candidate network.}
\looseness=-1 The four training seeds of Per-candidate + IDM averaged
\corlBenchMlpMean\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}a), about the trot's
\corlBenchTrot\%, and ranged from \corlBenchMlpMin{} to
\corlBenchMlpMax\%. GaitNet beat all four, the best by less than two points,
within run-to-run variation. The four spread over
twice GaitNet's gap to their mean. Since GaitNet's other training seeds were not
run on OCS2, the benchmark cannot rank the two networks, which also differ
in candidate mixer, history, and size (\corlParamsGaitNetK{}k against
\corlParamsPerCandidateK{}k parameters).
