\section{Simulation Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We ask whether GaitNet beats OCS2's own trot
(\textbf{Q1}), what it loses in moving to OCS2
(\textbf{Q2}), and how much it needs the IDM, overlapping swings, non-diagonal pairs,
history, and mixer (\textbf{Q3}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:exp-setup}

\noindent\textbf{Benchmark.}
\looseness=-1 The benchmark runs on OCS2 in Gazebo
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-deployment}). Each world is a strip of
\corlBenchTileCm\,cm tiles, the training tile size, each a solid pillar
or, with probability equal to the void fraction, a void. The void
fractions, \corlBenchVoidLow, \corlBenchVoidMid, and \corlBenchVoidHigh\%,
are the void areas of Isaac's worlds at nominal void fractions of
\corlBenchIsaacVoidLow, \corlBenchIsaacVoidMid, and
\corlBenchIsaacVoidHigh\%, and the commanded speeds are
\corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s. Each of these six
combinations, a \emph{cell}, holds \corlBenchTrialsPerCell{} trials of
\corlBenchTrialSeconds\,s on world seeds
\corlBenchSeedFirst--\corlBenchSeedLast, so each run of a method covers
\corlBenchTrialsPerArm{} trials, on the same worlds as every other
method. A trial succeeds if
the robot does not fall and walks at least half the commanded distance.
None of these world seeds was used to log the OCS2 IDM's data or to
develop the depth pipeline.

\noindent\textbf{GaitNet's checkpoint.}
\looseness=-1 We evaluate one of \corlNumSeedsGaitNet{} runs trained
from scratch (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}) with different training
seeds, picked at random, at iteration \corlOcsCheckpoint, its best
checkpoint on the training controller in Isaac, fixed before any trial
on OCS2. It took about \corlTrainHours\,h to train on one NVIDIA RTX PRO
6000 Blackwell GPU. Only this training seed ran on OCS2. For Q2, it also runs on the training controller in Isaac at
matching nominal void fractions, with \corlBenchIsaacRobots{} robots per
cell, one trial each, and the same criterion.

\noindent\textbf{Methods (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}).}
\looseness=-1 All methods share the depth pipeline, NMPC, and WBC.
\emph{GaitNet} runs as deployed, with the OCS2 IDM and up to two legs
swinging in any pair. \emph{OCS2's own trot} is
OCS2's perceptive trot, which lifts each diagonal pair (a front leg and the opposite rear leg) on a fixed schedule,
with its own footholds, \corlTrotSwing\,s swings, and a
\corlTrotPeriod\,s period. \emph{GaitNet-Diagonal} uses GaitNet's weights
but starts a second swing only on the diagonal partner of the swinging
leg. \emph{Per-candidate + IDM} is the per-candidate network of
\citet{Sullivan2025} (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}). We trained
four networks, one per training seed, with GaitNet's recipe but without
the IDM in the loop, for about \corlBenchMlpIterations{} iterations, more
than GaitNet's \corlOcsCheckpoint, and ran each with the OCS2 IDM.
\emph{GaitNet without the IDM} (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}a) uses
GaitNet's weights and sends its desired foothold unchanged.

\noindent\textbf{Retrained ablations (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}b).}
\looseness=-1 We retrained GaitNet under a separate training recipe,
with two training seeds per version, and ran each on the benchmark. \emph{Full retrain} keeps every part, while \emph{No
history}, \emph{No candidate mixer}, and \emph{No IDM in training} each
drop one part. Since their recipe differs from GaitNet's, the
full retrain is the reference for the others. No IDM in
training runs on OCS2 twice, with no IDM and with the training
controller's (Isaac) IDM, and the others with \verify{the OCS2 IDM}.

\noindent\textbf{Sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}).}
% Author, 2026-11-04: same checkpoint, same worlds for every method,
% 12.5 cm tiles, 80 trials per point; GaitNet-Single trained one-swing.
\looseness=-1 The sweep is a second set of trials with the same
checkpoint and \corlBenchTileCm\,cm tiles, at eight nominal void
fractions (0 to \corlOcsMaxVoidPct\%; above \corlMaxVoidPct\%, beyond the
training curriculum) and five speeds (\corlOcsSpeedMin{} to
\corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s), every method on the same worlds. Each method runs
\corlOcsTrialsPerPoint{} trials per void fraction, spread over the
speeds; GaitNet, GaitNet-Single, and the trot also run
\corlOcsTrialsPerPoint{} per speed, 10 at each void fraction. A trial succeeds if the robot reaches the end of a
\corlOcsTrackM\,m track within \corlOcsTimeFactor{} times the time needed
at the commanded speed. Besides GaitNet and the trot, the sweep runs
GaitNet without the IDM, Per-candidate + IDM with one training seed, and
\emph{GaitNet-Single}, trained with GaitNet's training seed and recipe
but with only one leg allowed to swing at a time.

\noindent\textbf{Variation.}
\looseness=-1 Brackets give 95\% intervals over the trials of one
checkpoint, for success rates \citep{wilson_probable_1927} and for gaps
between two methods \citep{newcombe_interval_1998}. They treat trials as
independent and omit training seeds, which can differ far more, as the
per-candidate network's four did (\corlBenchMlpMin{} to
\corlBenchMlpMax\%). Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}b therefore shows
the mean of two, and we conclude only from differences both share.

\subsection{Results}
\label{subsec:exp-transfer}

\noindent\textbf{Q1. Against OCS2's own trot.}
\looseness=-1 On the benchmark (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}c), GaitNet succeeded
in \corlBenchFull\% [89.0, 93.5] of trials against the trot's
\corlBenchTrot\% [80.9, 86.7]. The gap is 7.5 percentage points
[3.8, 11.2]. Since both methods share the terrain, NMPC, and WBC, the gap comes from
the planner and execution layer together, not from free leg pairing alone
(Q3). Per cell
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}a,\,b), GaitNet beat the trot by only 2 to 6 points at
\corlBenchVoidLow{} and \corlBenchVoidMid\% voids but by 16 and 14
points at \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids. There, over both speeds, it
succeeded in \corlBenchFullDense\% [81.1, 90.6] against
\corlBenchTrotDense\% [64.9, 77.3], so two
thirds of the gap came from \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids. We
conjecture that the trot's fixed schedule can force a step with no safe
foothold in reach, while GaitNet can wait, move another leg, or step
farther.

\looseness=-1 The sweep showed the same trend over a wider range
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}). GaitNet succeeded in \corlSweepFull\% [79.5,
85.4] of its trials against the trot's \corlSweepTrot\% [60.3, 67.7]. From
30\% voids on, it beat the trot by 15 to 42.5 points, with
\corlOcsDenseFull\% against \corlOcsDenseTrot\% success over
40--\corlOcsMaxVoidPct\% voids, partly beyond its training curriculum, and it beat the trot at every speed, by 5
to 17.5 points. Its success stayed between 81 and 89\% up to 0.20\,m/s
and fell to 66\% at \corlOcsSpeedMax\,m/s, above its training range.

\noindent\textbf{Q2. From the training controller to OCS2.}
\looseness=-1 The frozen GaitNet kept most of its success in moving to
OCS2 (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}a), with \corlBenchFull\% against
\corlBenchIsaacIdm\% [94.3, 97.2] on the training controller in Isaac. Most of the gap
came at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s (\corlBenchFullFast{} against 94.7\%).
Per cell (App.~\ref{app:counts}), it lost at most 8 points except in the
hardest cell, \corlBenchVoidHigh\% voids at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s,
where it lost 12. The gap combines all changes of Problem~2, which we do not
separate. As the checkpoint was its run's best in Isaac, its Isaac
success may be optimistic.

\noindent\phantomsection\label{subsec:exp-why}\textbf{Q3. IDM.}
\looseness=-1 Without the IDM (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}a), the
frozen GaitNet's success fell to \corlBenchNoIdm\% on OCS2, far below the
trot, and to \corlBenchIsaacNoIdm\% on the training controller. In the
sweep, it succeeded in \corlSweepNoIdm\% without the IDM
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}a), more than on the benchmark at the same nominal
void fractions, a difference we have not explained. In a
separate flat-ground test without voids at \corlBenchSpeedSlow{} and
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s,
OCS2's landing error, as the RMS of its length, was
\corlOcsLandRmsNoIdm\,cm without the IDM, several times the
\corlMarginCm\,cm candidate margin, and \corlOcsLandRmsIdm\,cm with it,
equal to the margin. Removing the IDM
also changes GaitNet's inputs, since its history reports touchdown errors
relative to the IDM's output. This ablation therefore measures GaitNet's
dependence on the IDM, not the IDM's value alone.

% and 337/600 = 56.2 (both 768/1200 = 64.0 over all cells); full retrain

\looseness=-1 Retraining without the IDM asks a different question:
whether a policy trained without it does as well, not how much the trained
policy depends on it. Retrained and run without the IDM
(Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}b), GaitNet trailed the full retrain
at both speeds, with 76.8 and 51.2\% against 92 and 72.5\%, and both
its training seeds stayed below both of the full retrain's. Adding the training controller's IDM at deployment did
not help overall, as success fell five points at \corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s
and rose five at \corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s. As we did not run this version with
the OCS2 IDM, the comparison with the full retrain shows that training and
deploying with the IDM helped on OCS2, not that it must be in the
training loop.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Leg pairs and gait.}
\looseness=-1 In its successful trials (App.~\ref{app:gait}), GaitNet, free to pair
any legs, kept the trot's pairing but timed its steps on its own
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gait}). Of its overlapping swings,
\corlGaitDiagPairPct\% paired a front leg with the opposite rear leg, and
these partners lifted off a median \corlGaitLiftLagMs\,ms apart, about
one tick (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-step}). The time
between a leg's lift-offs varied far more than the trot's (coefficient
of variation \corlGaitStrideCv{} against \corlGaitStrideCvTrot).
GaitNet-Diagonal succeeded in \corlBenchDiag\%
[84.3, 89.6] (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}c), 4.3 points [0.8, 7.9] below
GaitNet. Most of the gap came from the hardest cell, with 59\% against
77\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}b). Over all cells, GaitNet-Diagonal
stayed about three points [$-0.8$, 7.2] above the trot. Never trained
with this restriction, GaitNet may rely on non-diagonal pairs on dense
voids at the faster speed; free pairing need not be better in general.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. Overlapping swings.}
\looseness=-1 Trained to swing one leg at a time, GaitNet-Single
succeeded in \corlSweepSingle\% [55.7, 63.3] of the sweep's trials
against GaitNet's \corlSweepFull\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}), less often at
every void fraction and speed. It took shorter steps,
\corlOcsStepsPerMSingle{} per metre against \corlOcsStepsPerMFull{} for
GaitNet in successful trials. Its one swing at a time, of at least
\corlSwingDurationMin\,s and started on ticks, caps it near eight steps
per second, too few only from about 0.20\,m/s, so the cap cannot explain
its 21-point gap at 0.10\,m/s. With one training
seed each, this suggests that overlapping swings helped on these voids.

\noindent\textbf{Q3. History and candidate mixer.}
\looseness=-1 Retrained without the history
(Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-ablations}b), GaitNet matched the full retrain at
\corlBenchSpeedSlow\,m/s (91.2 against 92\%) but fell well behind at
\corlBenchSpeedFast\,m/s (52.8 against 72.5\%), where both its training
seeds stayed below both of the full retrain's. The retrained GaitNet thus needed the
history only at the faster speed, for unmeasured reasons. Without the
mixer, the two training seeds disagreed, matching the full retrain and
falling below it, so the mixer's effect is open.

% GaitNet minus the best per-candidate seed: 91.5 - 89.7 = 1.8 points.
% Seed spread 89.7 - 75.5 = 14.2 points; GaitNet minus the seed mean 7.0.
\noindent\textbf{Per-candidate network.}
\looseness=-1 The four training seeds of Per-candidate + IDM averaged
\corlBenchMlpMean\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}c), about the trot's
\corlBenchTrot\%, ranging from \corlBenchMlpMin{} to
\corlBenchMlpMax\%. GaitNet beat all four, the best by under two points
[$-1.5$, 5.2]. With one GaitNet training seed on OCS2,
the benchmark cannot rank the two networks, which also differ
in mixer, history, size (App.~\ref{app:settings}), and the IDM in
training.
