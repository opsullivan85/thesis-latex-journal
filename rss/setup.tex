\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Setting.}
A Unitree Go1 walks forward at a commanded velocity over level ground
with voids, the set $\mathcal V$. A planner $\pi$ decides the steps, and a
model-based controller $c$ executes them. The planner is queried at
ticks $t_k = k\,\Delta t$. It
is given no \emph{gait schedule}, a map from a phase variable to the legs
in stance (on the ground), as an input or a constraint.

\noindent\textbf{Decisions.}
\looseness=-1 At each tick, the planner takes the no-step option $\varnothing$ or
starts a step \mbox{$a_k = (\ell, \mathbf u^\star, T)$}, which lifts leg
$\ell$ and should land it at the desired foothold $\mathbf u^\star$ after
a swing of duration $T \in [T_{\min}, T_{\max}]$. Since the step starts at
the tick it is chosen, one decision fixes which leg steps, where, for how
long, and when. Leg $\ell$ may step
only if at least \numberstringnum{\corlMinContactLegs} legs, $\ell$ among
them, are in stance in the \emph{contact schedule} set by the planner's
earlier steps. A chosen leg that the controller has not yet lifted,
as it may lift late, counts as lifted. Hence at most \numberstringnum{\corlMaxConcurrentSwingLegs} legs
swing at once: without a gait schedule, contacts are still constrained.
The foothold $\mathbf u^\star$ must be one of the leg's
candidates $\mathcal C_k^\ell$, cells near the hip drawn at random from
those that a terrain mask with candidate margin $m$ marks as safe
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-step}). The planner observes the robot's state,
a short window of its recent motion (the history), and the
candidates (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-network}). It sees neither the rest
of the terrain nor the controller's internal state, so the decision
process is partially observed, with the controller as part of the
environment \citep{yang_fast_2021}. A frozen planner must therefore
infer from the history how the controller in use executes its steps.

\noindent\textbf{Execution.}
\looseness=-1 Given a \emph{command} $\mathbf u$, the foothold sent for
leg $\ell$, and a swing time $T$, controller $c$ lands the foot at
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf o = F_c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u)
    = \mathbf u + \boldsymbol\delta_c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u),
  \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf u, \mathbf o \in \mathbb R^2$ and $\mathbf s$ is
everything that affects the swing: the robot's state, the other legs,
the terrain, and the controller's internal state. Footholds, commands,
landings, and $\mathcal V$ are planar offsets from the stepping leg's hip
at $t_k$, in the gravity-aligned \emph{yaw frame} that points along the
robot's heading at $t_k$. The \emph{landing offset}
$\boldsymbol\delta_c$ includes the base's motion between the decision and
touchdown. In general $\boldsymbol\delta_c \neq \mathbf 0$, and it
differs between controllers (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}), so sending
the desired foothold unchanged, $\mathbf u = \mathbf u^\star$, can put
the foot in a void although $\mathbf u^\star$ is safe. An
\emph{execution layer} between planner and controller guards against
this. Its IDM $I^c$, fitted to controller $c$, sends the command
$\mathbf u = I^c(\zeta, \mathbf u^\star)$ expected to land the foot at
$\mathbf u^\star$, where $\zeta = (\mathbf h_{\text{last}}, \ell, T)$
holds the newest tick of the history, the leg, and the swing time.

\noindent\textbf{Problem 1 (planning on the training controller).}
\looseness=-1 The planner is trained with the \emph{training controller} $c_0$, the
convex MPC of \citet{dicarlo_dynamic_2018}. The controller's parameters, including its lift-off delay, are
redrawn at every reset (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}). With
$I^{c_0}$ fitted beforehand and frozen, we seek the planner that
maximizes the expected discounted return $\mathbb E[\sum_{k \ge 0}
\gamma^k r_k]$ when $c_0$ executes its
steps on random void worlds and commanded velocities. The reward $r_k$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:reward}) pays for staying up and tracking the commanded
velocity and yaw rate, and charges for each step and failure. A
failure, such as a foot dropping into a void, ends the episode, and $r_k
= 0$ after it. A time-out is not a failure
(App.~\ref{app:training}). Since $r_k$ has no void-clearance, landing,
or track-completion term, the return is only a proxy for trial success
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-setup}).

\noindent\textbf{Problem 2 (deployment on a new controller).}
\looseness=-1 The \emph{new controller} $c_1$ is OCS2's NMPC and WBC.
The planner is frozen, and only $I^{c_1}$ is fitted, by regression on steps that $c_1$ executed
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}). The goal is that the planner succeeds on
held-out worlds (Sec.~\ref{subsec:exp-setup}) at a rate comparable to or
above that of $c_1$ with its own gait. Moving to $c_1$ also changes the
simulator, odometry, and terrain source (a depth camera). No planner
setting is tuned for $c_1$: the hand-set candidate margin $m$ stays the
same on the depth map that all methods share (App.~\ref{app:deployment}).
