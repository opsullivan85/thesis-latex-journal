\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Setting.}
\looseness=-1 A Unitree Go1 walks forward at a given speed over level
ground with voids. A planner, queried every tick of
$\Delta t = \corlControlTickMs$\,ms, decides the steps, and a
model-based controller $c$ executes them. The planner is given no
gait schedule %, a map from a gait phase to the legs in stance, 
as an input or constraint.

\noindent\textbf{Decisions.}
\looseness=-1 At each tick, the planner takes the no-step option
$\varnothing$ or starts one step \mbox{$(\ell, \mathbf u^\star, T)$},
which lifts leg $\ell$ and should land it at the \emph{desired foothold}
$\mathbf u^\star$ after a swing time $T \in [T_{\min}, T_{\max}] =
[\corlSwingDurationMin, \corlSwingDurationMax]$\,s. %Since the step starts at the tick it is
%chosen, one decision fixes which leg steps, where, for how long, and
%when. 
No more than 2 legs may be in swing at once,
%A leg may start a step only if at least
%\numberstringnum{\corlMinContactLegs} legs, itself among them, are in
%stance in the \emph{contact schedule} set by earlier steps, so at most
%\numberstringnum{\corlMaxConcurrentSwingLegs} legs swing at once. 
and the desired foothold must be one of the leg's \emph{candidates}
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-step}). The planner sees the robot's state, a
\corlHistSeconds\,s history, and the candidates, but neither the %rest of the 
terrain nor the controller's internal state, so the decision process
is partially observed \citep{yang_fast_2021}.

\noindent\textbf{Closed-loop footstep map.}
\looseness=-1 Given a \emph{command} $\mathbf u$, the foothold sent for
leg $\ell$, and a swing time $T$, the controller lands the foot at
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf o = F_c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u)
    = \mathbf u + \boldsymbol\delta_c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u),
  \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf s$ is everything else that affects the swing. Footholds,
commands, landings, and voids are planar offsets from the stepping
leg's hip at the decision tick, in the gravity-aligned \emph{yaw frame}
along the robot's heading at that tick. Since this frame stays fixed in
the world after the decision tick, the \emph{landing offset}
$\boldsymbol\delta_c$ also contains whatever part of the base's motion
until touchdown the controller passes on to the foot. It is
a property of the controller and differs between controllers, whereas
the \emph{landing error} $\mathbf o - \mathbf u^\star$ compares the
landing with the desired foothold. The execution layer therefore sends
$\mathbf u = I^c(\zeta, \mathbf u^\star)$, where the IFM $I^c$ is meant
to invert $F_c$ and $\zeta$ holds the newest tick of the history, the
leg, and the swing time. The planner has no controller-specific
parameter, but its history reflects the controller, so its inputs can
still shift between controllers.

\noindent\textbf{Problems.}
\looseness=-1 \emph{Problem~1:} with the \emph{training controller}, the
convex MPC of \citet{dicarlo_dynamic_2018} randomized at every reset,
and its IFM fitted beforehand and frozen, find the planner that
maximizes $\mathbb E[\sum_{k} \gamma^k r_k]$, with discount $\gamma$
and the reward function in Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}. \emph{Problem~2:} on the \emph{new controller},
OCS2's NMPC and WBC, keep the planner frozen and fit only its IFM, on
fewer than 20{,}000 OCS2 steps, so that the planner succeeds on held-out
worlds at least as often as OCS2's own gaits. Moving to OCS2 also
changes the simulator, odometry, and terrain source (a depth camera),
and no planner setting is tuned for it (App.~\ref{app:deployment}).
