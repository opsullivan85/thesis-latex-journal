\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:setup}

A Unitree Go1 walks forward over ground with voids, following a
commanded velocity. All footholds, commands and landings below are
planar offsets from the leg's hip in the \emph{yaw frame}, the gravity-aligned frame that turns
with the robot's heading, taken at the moment of the decision.

\paragraph{Decisions.}
At ticks $t_k = k\,\Delta t$, $\Delta t = \corlControlTickMs$\,ms, a planner
$\pi$ observes the robot's state, a short history of its motion, and a
finite set $\mathcal C_k$ of candidate footholds that a safety mask
keeps, with exact terrain, at least 1.5\,cm from voids, and $m = \corlMarginCm$\,cm when the
void edges are parallel to its grid axes
(Secs.~\ref{subsec:method-step} and~\ref{subsec:idm-theory}). It returns either the no-step option
$\varnothing$ or a step $a_k = (\ell, \mathbf u^\star, T)$: a leg
$\ell$ that is not already swinging, a desired landing point
$\mathbf u^\star \in \mathcal C_k$, and a swing time
$T \in [T_{\min}, T_{\max}]$. At least
\numberstringnum{\corlMinStanceLegs} legs must stay in stance, so at
most \numberstringnum{\corlMaxConcurrentSwingLegs} legs swing at once.
A swing lasts several ticks, so the decision process is semi-Markov,
and it is partially observed: the planner sees the terrain only through
$\mathcal C_k$.

\paragraph{Controllers.}
A step is executed by a low-level controller $c$, here a model
predictive controller that tracks the body and swings the feet. It
receives a command $\mathbf u$ for leg $\ell$, a swing time $T$ and a
start time. From the planner's point of view, $c$ is a closed-loop map
from command to landing,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf o = F_c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u)
    = \mathbf u + \boldsymbol\delta_c(\mathbf s, \ell, T, \mathbf u),
  \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf u, \mathbf o \in \mathbb R^2$, $\mathbf s$ is everything that affects the swing (the robot's
state, the other legs, the terrain and the controller's internal state)
and $\boldsymbol\delta_c$ is the controller's landing error. Both
$\boldsymbol\delta_c$ and how the controller schedules a step differ
between controllers, and in general $\boldsymbol\delta_c \neq
\mathbf 0$ (Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-idm}). Sending $\mathbf u =
\mathbf u^\star$ therefore lands the foot at $\mathbf u^\star +
\boldsymbol\delta_c$, which can be inside a void although
$\mathbf u^\star$ is not.

\paragraph{Objective and transfer.}
A trial succeeds if the robot reaches the end of a track without
falling or leaving it, within a time limit. The planner is trained with a
\emph{training controller} $c_0$, the convex MPC of
\citet{dicarlo_dynamic_2018}, by reinforcement learning
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:method-training}). It is then deployed with a
\emph{target controller} $c_1$, OCS2's nonlinear MPC and whole-body
controller \citep{ocs2, leggedcontrol}, under one constraint: the
planner's weights are frozen, and the only learned part fitted for
$c_1$ is a map from desired landings to commands, learned from steps
that $c_1$ has executed; a few interface constants, such as the step
lead and the candidate margin, are set by hand. We ask whether the frozen planner then reaches, on held-out
worlds, a success rate comparable to or above that of $c_1$'s own gait.
